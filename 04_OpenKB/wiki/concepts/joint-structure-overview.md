# Overview of Joint Structures and How Cartilage Supports Cushioning and Motion

## Definition
This concept explains how the main structures of a joint work together to support movement, protection, and long-term joint function. Cartilage is central to the joint system because it cushions bone ends, reduces friction, and helps maintain smooth articulation. Its maintenance depends on a balance between matrix synthesis and degradation, especially through the interaction of [[concepts/proteoglycan|proteoglycans]], [[concepts/collagen|collagen]], [[concepts/chondrocyte|chondrocytes]], and the biochemical balance of [[concepts/mmp-timp-balance|MMPs and TIMPs]].

## Core joint components
A functioning joint depends on several structures that operate together:

- **Cartilage (연골)** — cushions the ends of bones and protects the joint surface
- **Synovial membrane (활막)** — lines the joint area and supports joint function
- **Synovial fluid (활액)** — reduces friction and supports smooth movement
- **Ligament (인대)** — connects and stabilizes the joint
- **Joint cavity (관절강)** — the space in which movement occurs

## Cartilage as a protective joint tissue
Cartilage is especially important because it protects articular surfaces and absorbs mechanical stress. It is described as:

- **Elastic**, allowing the joint to withstand pressure and movement
- **Avascular and aneural**, meaning it has no blood vessels or nerves
- A tissue that enables smooth contact between bones during motion

In the context of [[concepts/osteoarthritis]], damage to cartilage is a key feature of joint degeneration and pain.

## Cartilage composition and how it supports function
The internal structure of cartilage explains how it contributes to joint cushioning, lubrication, and maintenance.

### Collagen
- Cartilage contains **collagen**, especially **Type II collagen** in articular cartilage
- Collagen is a fibrous protein found in connective tissue such as skin, ligaments, and bone
- In cartilage, collagen primarily provides **structural strength and elasticity**

### Proteoglycans
- **Proteoglycans** are major components of the cartilage matrix
- They consist of a core protein with multiple **glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)** attached
- Examples of GAGs include:
  - Keratan sulfate
  - Chondroitin sulfate
- Proteoglycans form a mesh-like matrix with collagen that helps:
  - retain water and hydration
  - support resilience and shock absorption
  - reduce friction-like resistance during movement
  - maintain cartilage shape and mobility

### Chondrocytes
- **Chondrocytes** are cartilage cells
- They are responsible for the **growth, repair, and maintenance** of cartilage tissue
- Their activity helps preserve the cartilage matrix over time

## Functional relationship among components
Cartilage function depends on interaction among mechanical and biochemical elements rather than a single tissue alone:

- Cartilage reduces direct bone-to-bone contact
- Collagen provides structural support and elasticity
- Proteoglycans maintain hydration and shock-absorbing capacity
- Chondrocytes sustain cartilage growth and maintenance
- Synovial fluid supports lubrication
- Synovial membrane contributes to the joint environment
- Ligaments limit excessive movement and provide stability
- The joint cavity provides the physical space for articulation

Together, these structures form a coordinated system for movement, protection, and long-term joint maintenance.

## Biochemical maintenance and degeneration balance
Cartilage integrity is not static. It is maintained through a balance between synthesis and degradation. One source in this series highlights the role of **matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)**, **tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs)**, and inflammatory cytokines such as **IL-1** and **TNF-α**.

- **MMPs** break down cartilage matrix components
- **TIMPs** inhibit MMP activity
- **Cytokines** can promote MMP-mediated degradation
- When the balance between these factors is disrupted, cartilage tissue is damaged

This biochemical balance is important for understanding both cartilage maintenance and cartilage injury, especially in [[concepts/cartilage-degradation]] and related inflammatory processes.

## Joint inflammation and autoimmune damage
Joint structure can also be damaged by chronic inflammatory disease. In [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]], persistent **synovial inflammation** leads to **pannus** formation, where proliferating synovial tissue and infiltrating inflammatory cells invade adjacent cartilage and bone.

- **Inflammatory synovitis** can destroy cartilage and contribute to joint deformity
- **Pannus** is thickened synovial tissue that grows into and erodes cartilage
- Progressive damage may lead to **bone erosion**, **joint fusion**, and **stiffness**

This shows that the same structures that support smooth joint motion can also be the site of destructive pathology when inflammation becomes chronic.
