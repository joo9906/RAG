# Medication Storage and Handling

Medication storage and handling refers to the practices used to keep medicines safe, effective, and free from misuse or quality loss during storage and use. It includes where a drug is kept, how it is transferred between containers, and the environmental conditions maintained over time.

## Core principles

### 1. Keep medicines out of reach of children
Medicines should be stored in places that children cannot access. This reduces the risk of accidental ingestion and related harm.

### 2. Keep medicines in their original container
Drugs should remain in their original packaging whenever possible. Transferring them to another container can increase the risk of:
- accidental misuse,
- confusion about the product,
- loss of labeling information,
- reduced product quality.

### 3. Protect from light, moisture, and excessive heat
Medicines should generally be stored in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. These conditions help prevent degradation and preserve potency.

### 4. Close containers securely after use
Containers should be tightly closed after each use to limit exposure to air and moisture and to maintain product stability.

## Why this concept matters
Proper storage and handling are part of safe medication use. Even when a medicine is pharmacologically appropriate, poor storage can reduce effectiveness or increase the chance of medication errors. These practices are especially important for household medicines and for products kept over long periods.
