# Patient-Reported Outcome Measures

Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are assessment tools completed by patients to describe their own symptoms, function, and disease impact. Unlike imaging-based or clinician-scored endpoints, PROMs capture the patient’s lived experience of illness and treatment.

## Core idea

PROMs are especially important in musculoskeletal diseases because pain and functional limitation often matter as much as structural findings. They help quantify how a condition affects daily life from the patient’s perspective.

In osteoarthritis research, PROMs are commonly used to assess whether a treatment improves **pain**, **stiffness**, and **physical function** over time. This makes them a central outcome class in studies such as those involving [[concepts/osteoarthritis-clinical-outcomes]] and [[concepts/clinical-evidence]].

## Common examples

### K-WOMAC
- Full name: Korean Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index
- Purpose: assesses osteoarthritis-related **pain, stiffness, and physical function**
- Format: self-report questionnaire
- Item count: **24 questions**

K-WOMAC is a disease-specific PROM used widely in knee osteoarthritis research. It provides a structured way to measure how OA affects symptoms and function from the patient’s own perspective.

### VAS
- Full name: Visual Analog Scale
- Purpose: measures subjective pain intensity
- Range: **0 mm to 100 mm**
- Lower values indicate less pain

VAS is a simple patient-based pain scale often used alongside disease-specific PROMs like K-WOMAC. It is especially useful for tracking changes in pain after treatment.

## Example from the source documents

The source documents [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0023]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0033]] both highlight PROMs in osteoarthritis research.

- One document introduces K-WOMAC and VAS as outcome measures in OA studies.
- The newer document reports that **1 year of JOINS® treatment** led to significant improvements versus placebo in both **VAS** and **K-WOMAC**.

This is a clear example of PROMs being used to demonstrate a treatment benefit in terms of how patients feel and function.

## Why PROMs matter

PROMs are useful because they:

- capture symptoms that may not be visible on imaging
- reflect treatment benefit from the patient’s perspective
- allow comparison of pain and function across studies
- support evaluation of quality of life and daily functioning

## PROMs in OA/RA clinical research

In osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis studies, PROMs are often paired with imaging measures such as [[concepts/mri-based-joint-assessment]] and structural scores. This combination helps researchers evaluate both:

- **How the patient feels**: pain, stiffness, function
- **What the joint looks like**: cartilage, bone marrow, and degenerative changes

PROMs are patient-completed tools that measure the subjective burden of disease. In the source documents, VAS and K-WOMAC are the main examples, representing pain intensity and osteoarthritis-related function outcomes. The newer JOINS® study reinforces their role as key endpoints for evaluating meaningful clinical improvement.
