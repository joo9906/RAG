# Inflammatory Markers

Inflammatory markers are blood tests used to estimate the presence and degree of inflammation in the body. In clinical practice, they are especially useful for assessing **disease activity**, supporting diagnosis, and monitoring response to treatment.

## Core markers

### ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate)
- Measures how quickly red blood cells settle in a tube over time.
- A higher ESR generally suggests **ongoing inflammation**.
- It is a **nonspecific** marker, meaning it can rise in many different inflammatory or systemic conditions.
- In some diseases, ESR may be elevated **transiently** rather than persistently.

### CRP (C-reactive Protein)
- A protein produced by the liver in response to inflammation.
- CRP increases when inflammation is active and often reflects **acute inflammatory change**.
- Like ESR, it is not disease-specific, but it is useful for tracking inflammatory burden.

## Role in rheumatoid arthritis

In rheumatoid disease, ESR and CRP are part of the inflammatory assessment used alongside symptoms, exam findings, and serologic tests. They are especially helpful in distinguishing inflammatory arthritis such as [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] from degenerative joint disease such as [[concepts/osteoarthritis]].

Key points from the source documents:
- In [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]], **ESR and CRP are typically elevated**.
- RA is characterized by **synovitis**, **systemic inflammation**, and a more rapid disease course than OA.
- In contrast, [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] may show **ESR that is normal or only temporarily elevated**, reflecting a more localized degenerative process.

## Why they matter

Inflammatory markers help clinicians:
- assess whether inflammation is active
- support diagnosis alongside symptoms and other tests
- monitor changes over time
- evaluate response to therapy
- help differentiate **inflammatory** from **non-inflammatory** joint disease

## Important limitations

- ESR and CRP are **not specific** to a single disease.
- Normal values do not always exclude disease.
- Results must be interpreted together with the clinical picture and other tests such as [[concepts/autoantibody-testing]] and routine laboratory monitoring.
