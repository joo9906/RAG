# Degenerative Joint Disease

## Definition
Degenerative joint disease (DJD) is another name for **[[concepts/osteoarthritis]]**, a chronic degenerative condition marked by progressive joint deterioration, pain, and structural change over time. In clinical and educational materials, DJD is commonly used interchangeably with osteoarthritis, including [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0005]], [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0007]], [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0009]], [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]], and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0038]].

## Core Idea
DJD refers to the gradual breakdown of joint structures, especially the **articular cartilage** that covers the ends of bones in a joint. As cartilage wears away, underlying bone becomes exposed, surrounding tissues may become inflamed, and the joint can become painful, stiff, swollen, and deformed.

The condition may affect many joints throughout the body, not only the knee. The new source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0038]] highlights that osteoarthritis can also occur in the **spine**, **hip**, and **finger joints**, which is important when considering treatment coverage and indication scope for products such as [[concepts/indication-coverage-comparison]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0038]].

## Key Clinical Features
The typical presentation of DJD/OA includes:

- **Activity-related joint pain**
- **Short-duration morning stiffness**: usually less than 30 minutes
- **Limited range of motion**
- **Joint swelling**
- **Joint sounds** such as crepitus
- **Joint deformity** in more advanced disease

These features reflect the mechanical and degenerative nature of the condition and help distinguish it from inflammatory arthritis patterns. This symptom profile is closely related to [[concepts/joint pain]], [[concepts/joint stiffness]], and [[concepts/limited range of motion]].

## Disease Pattern
DJD/OA may be classified as either **primary** or **secondary**.

### Primary osteoarthritis
- The exact cause is not clearly known.
- Likely influenced by:
  - age
  - sex
  - genetic factors
  - obesity

### Secondary osteoarthritis
- Results from damage to joint cartilage caused by:
  - **trauma**
  - **disease**
  - **deformity** or structural abnormality
- Representative examples include:
  - cartilage destruction after **bacterial arthritis**
  - cartilage destruction after **tuberculous arthritis**
  - joint degeneration after severe impact
  - OA caused by repeated minor trauma over time

## Mechanism
1. Joint cartilage gradually wears down or is damaged.
2. The bone beneath the cartilage becomes exposed.
3. Surrounding joint tissues, including the **synovial membrane**, may become inflamed.
4. The joint develops pain, stiffness, swelling, and structural change over time.
5. In secondary cases, earlier trauma or infection can initiate the degenerative process.

## Treatment Overview
Management of DJD focuses on relieving symptoms, preserving function, and improving quality of life. The document on OA treatment emphasizes three main goals:

- reduce joint pain
- maintain joint function and prevent deformity
- improve the patient’s quality of life

### Non-Pharmacologic Treatment
Non-drug therapy is a core part of treatment and includes:

- exercise
- weight control
- muscle strengthening

These interventions are considered foundational for OA care and are central to [[concepts/osteoarthritis-management]].

### Pharmacologic Treatment
Common medication options include:

- **Topical agents**: NSAIDs and related preparations
- **Oral agents**: acetaminophen, NSAIDs, COX-2 inhibitors
- **Intra-articular injections**: hyaluronic acid, steroids

Current therapies mainly target symptoms rather than the underlying disease process.

## Limits of Current Therapy
The document notes that, although non-pharmacologic and pharmacologic options can reduce symptoms, **no medication has yet been proven to prevent or reverse the structural degenerative changes** associated with OA progression.

This makes DJD a condition where treatment is largely symptom-focused, with an unmet need for true disease modification in [[concepts/disease-modifying-osteoarthritis-drugs]].

## OA Drug Classifications
The document also organizes OA drugs into three categories:

### SYRADOA
**Symptomatic Rapid Acting Drugs in OA**
- acetaminophen
- NSAIDs
- intra-articular corticosteroids

### SYSADOA
**Symptomatic Slow Acting Drugs in OA**
- glucosamine/chondroitin sulfate
- avocado/soybean unsaponifiables
- diacerin
- **Joins®**

These are slower-acting symptomatic treatments and are relevant to the therapeutic context in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0009]].

### DMOADs
**Disease Modifying OA Drugs**
- drugs that may reduce or alter structural joint changes

DMOADs remain a desired category because structural disease modification has not yet been established in routine care.

## Joins® in the Context of DJD
A new document, [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0038]], clarifies the practical relevance of DJD/OA by comparing **Imotion®** and **Joins®**.

### Indication and reimbursement scope
- **Imotion®** is described as having an indication limited to **knee osteoarthritis**.
- **Joins®** is presented as being reimbursable for **degenerative joint disease throughout the body**, including **spinal osteoarthritis**.

This comparison is clinically important because DJD is not restricted to the knee; it can involve multiple joints, and treatment access may depend on the joint affected. The source therefore reinforces the broader disease concept and the reimbursement distinction captured in [[concepts/indication-coverage-comparison]].

## Safety Context from Joins® Post-Marketing Surveillance
The source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]] adds real-world safety information for **Joins® (조인스정)**, a SYSADOA used in OA/DJD.

### Post-marketing surveillance results
- Surveillance period: **2001.07.10–2005.07.09**
- Population: **5,962 patients**
- Overall adverse event incidence, regardless of causality: **3.02% (180/5,962)**
- Adverse events that could not be ruled out as drug-related: **2.38% (142/5,962)**

### Most common adverse reactions
The most frequently reported events were **gastrointestinal**:
- heartburn
- dyspepsia
- epigastric discomfort
- gastrointestinal disorder
- epigastric pain

Other reported reactions were less common and included:
- edema
- headache
- pruritus
- rash
- leukopenia
- insomnia
- palpitations
- AST/ALT elevation
- dizziness

This supports the broader understanding that OA therapies such as Joins® are primarily **symptom-relieving agents** and may have adverse effects that require monitoring, especially **digestive system side effects**.

## Clinical Significance
DJD is a useful term for recognizing the chronic, progressive, and mechanical character of osteoarthritis. The key symptom pattern is **pain with use**, **brief morning stiffness**, and **loss of function** over time. The presence of joint sounds, swelling, and deformity supports the diagnosis of a degenerative process rather than an acute inflammatory one.

The inclusion of Joins® safety data and the broader reimbursement comparison in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0038]] highlights how OA management combines disease understanding with medication selection, coverage considerations, and adverse reaction monitoring in [[concepts/drug-safety]] and [[concepts/post-marketing-surveillance]].

## Notes
In clinical usage, DJD and OA are often used interchangeably, though OA is the more common modern term. The condition may be primary/idiopathic or secondary to prior cartilage damage, infection, trauma, or deformity. The symptom profile most often includes activity-related pain, brief morning stiffness, reduced motion, swelling, joint sounds, and possible deformity.
