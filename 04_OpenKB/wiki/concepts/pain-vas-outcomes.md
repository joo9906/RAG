# Pain VAS Outcomes

## Definition
Pain VAS outcomes refer to changes in **visual analogue scale (VAS)** scores used to quantify pain intensity over time in clinical studies. Lower scores indicate less pain, and reductions from baseline suggest treatment benefit.

## Why it matters
VAS is a common endpoint in pain and inflammation trials because it provides a simple, patient-reported measure of symptom improvement. It is often used to compare the effectiveness of treatments when evaluating [[concepts/clinical-trial-evidence]] and [[concepts/analgesic-equivalence]].

## How it is used
VAS outcomes can capture both:
- **Within-group change**: whether pain improves after treatment
- **Between-group difference**: whether one treatment reduces pain more than another

Because it is a continuous scale, VAS is useful for detecting relatively small changes in symptom burden over time.

## Example from the JOINS RA study
The source document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0039]] reports a phase 3 randomized controlled trial in rheumatoid arthritis comparing **JOINS** with **celecoxib** using VAS as the primary pain endpoint.

### Key findings
- **Study type:** RCT
- **Participants:** RA patients, **n = 183**
- **Intervention:**
  - **JOINS 200 mg tid**
  - **Celecoxib 200 mg bid**
- **Duration:** 6 weeks
- **Primary endpoint:** VAS
- **Secondary endpoints:** rescue medication use frequency, 20% improvement response rate
- Both treatments showed pain reduction, and the **pain-relieving effect of JOINS was non-inferior to celecoxib**.
- **Tolerability was similar** between the two groups.

This supports VAS as a useful measure for demonstrating comparative analgesic benefit in [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] treatment studies.

## Example from the JOINS OA study
The source document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0033]] reports a phase 4 osteoarthritis study evaluating JOINS® versus placebo over 1 year.

### Key findings
- JOINS® showed **significant improvements** versus placebo in both **VAS** and **K-WOMAC**.
- The 1-year JOINS® group demonstrated better outcomes in **pain** and **physical function**.
- This supports the use of VAS as a long-term indicator of treatment-related pain reduction in osteoarthritis.

## Interpretation
In these studies, VAS outcomes suggest that **JOINS provided meaningful pain relief** in both rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis settings. In the RA trial, the effect was **comparable to celecoxib**; in the OA trial, the effect was **better than placebo** over long-term use. This makes VAS a useful metric for detecting both absolute symptom improvement and comparative treatment effects.
