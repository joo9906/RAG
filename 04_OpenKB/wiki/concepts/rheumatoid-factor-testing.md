# Rheumatoid Factor Testing

**Rheumatoid factor (RF) testing** is a serologic blood test used in the evaluation of **rheumatoid arthritis (RA)**. It supports diagnosis and classification, but it is **not definitive on its own** because RF can also be positive in people without RA.

## What it is

Rheumatoid factor is an antibody-related marker measured in blood. In practice, it is interpreted together with clinical findings, symptom pattern, and other laboratory tests.

## Role in RA diagnosis

According to [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0013]], RA diagnosis is based on a point system across several categories:

1. Joint involvement pattern  
2. Serologic testing  
3. Acute-phase reactants such as [[concepts/inflammatory-markers|ESR]] and [[concepts/inflammatory-markers|CRP]]  
4. Duration of symptoms  

A score of **6 or more** supports an RA diagnosis.

RF belongs to the **serologic test** category and contributes to this overall assessment.

## Key interpretation points

- RF is positive in about **80% of RA patients**.
- RF can also be positive in about **5% of healthy individuals**.
- Therefore, **RF positivity alone does not confirm RA**.
- Higher RF levels are associated with a tendency toward **more severe joint damage**.

## Distinguishing RA from osteoarthritis

A later source, [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0015]], contrasts [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] with [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] and reinforces the clinical value of RF:

- In **OA**, RF is typically **negative**.
- In **RA**, RF is typically **positive**.
- OA is a **degenerative** disease with gradual progression, while RA is an **autoimmune inflammatory** disease with faster progression.
- RA commonly presents with **symmetric**, **small-joint** involvement and prolonged morning stiffness, whereas OA is often **asymmetric** and affects **weight-bearing joints**.

This makes RF especially useful when the clinical picture suggests inflammatory arthritis rather than degenerative joint disease.

## Clinical significance

RF testing is useful for:

- supporting suspicion of RA
- helping classify patients within RA diagnostic criteria
- estimating possible disease severity when RF is elevated
- helping differentiate RA from [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] in the appropriate clinical context

However, RF should always be interpreted alongside:

- joint symptoms and exam findings
- [[concepts/inflammatory-markers|ESR]] and [[concepts/inflammatory-markers|CRP]]
- other routine labs such as CBC, liver function tests, and kidney function tests
- the overall pattern of joint involvement and symptom duration

## Practical takeaway

RF is an important but **non-specific** marker. It is best understood as one piece of the diagnostic picture rather than a standalone test. Its main value is in supporting [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis-diagnosis]] and distinguishing RA from other joint disorders when combined with clinical assessment.
