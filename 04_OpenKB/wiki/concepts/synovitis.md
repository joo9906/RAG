# Synovitis

[[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0011]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0012]] show synovitis as the central inflammatory process in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), while [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0013]] adds the diagnostic and laboratory context used to identify and track the disease. [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0014]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0015]] expand the management and differential-diagnosis picture by showing how treatment targets inflammation driven by synovitis and how RA differs from [[concepts/osteoarthritis]].

## Definition

**Synovitis** is inflammation of the **synovial membrane**, the tissue lining the joint capsule that produces synovial fluid.

In inflammatory arthritis, synovitis is not a brief swelling response. It can become **chronic, persistent, and self-sustaining**, leading to progressive injury inside the joint.

## Role in rheumatoid arthritis

Synovitis is a core feature of [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] and helps explain both local joint symptoms and longer-term damage.

RA commonly affects the **small joints of the hands, wrists, and feet**, but it can also involve the elbows, shoulders, hips, knees, and ankles. Active synovitis in these joints causes:

- pain
- swelling
- tenderness
- reduced joint mobility
- [[concepts/morning-stiffness|morning stiffness]], often lasting more than 30 minutes and sometimes all day

The disease may also begin abruptly in some patients, with systemic symptoms such as:

- fever
- polyarthritis
- fatigue
- depression or anxiety
- poor appetite
- weight loss

These findings reinforce RA as a systemic inflammatory disease rather than a purely localized joint disorder.

## Pathological significance

Chronic synovitis contributes to joint destruction through several mechanisms:

- **Synovial thickening** due to cellular proliferation
- **Inflammatory cell infiltration** into the synovium
- Formation of **pannus**, an invasive overgrowth of inflamed synovial tissue
- Extension of pannus into cartilage and bone
- Resulting **erosion** and irreversible joint damage

Over time, persistent inflammation can progress to **cartilage destruction**, **joint stiffness**, and even **fusion**.

## Relationship to pannus

In RA, prolonged synovitis can lead to **pannus formation**. Pannus is a thickened, invasive synovial tissue mass that grows into nearby structures and accelerates joint destruction. This makes synovitis a central upstream process in [[concepts/pannus]] and [[concepts/joint-destruction]].

## Diagnostic and laboratory context

The diagnostic framework for RA is used alongside the clinical suspicion of synovitis.

RA diagnosis is supported by scoring across four categories:

1. **Joint involvement pattern**
2. **Serologic tests**
3. **Acute-phase reactants** such as ESR and CRP
4. **Symptom duration**

A total of **6 points or more** supports a diagnosis of RA.

### Rheumatoid factor

- **Rheumatoid factor (RF)** is positive in about **80%** of RA patients.
- About **5% of healthy individuals** may also test positive.
- Therefore, RF positivity alone does **not** confirm RA.
- Higher RF levels are associated with a tendency toward **more severe joint damage**.

### Inflammatory markers

- **ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate)** and **CRP (C-reactive protein)** are used to assess the **degree of inflammation**.
- These values are often elevated when inflammatory disease is active.

### Differentiation from osteoarthritis

[[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0015]] highlights how RA-related synovitis differs from the degenerative process in [[concepts/osteoarthritis]]. Compared with OA, RA is more likely to show:

- younger onset, often **20–40 years**
- **autoimmune** origin with primary synovial inflammation
- **rapid** progression
- morning stiffness lasting **1 hour or more**
- **symmetric** joint involvement
- involvement of **small joints** such as fingers and wrists
- **RF positivity** and elevated **ESR/CRP**

By contrast, OA is more associated with age-related cartilage damage, osteophytes, asymmetry, and weight-bearing joints.

### Routine safety labs

- **CBC, liver function tests, and kidney function tests** help assess:
  - treatment adverse effects
  - other organ involvement

These tests complement the clinical assessment of synovitis by showing disease activity and monitoring therapy safety.

## Therapeutic significance

Synovitis is a key treatment target in [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]]. The treatment overview in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0014]] shows the main medication classes used to control the inflammation that synovitis reflects and sustains:

- **NSAIDs** to reduce pain and inflammation
- **Steroids** for strong anti-inflammatory effect
- **DMARDs** such as methotrexate, sulfasalazine, and leflunomide to slow disease progression and induce remission
- **Biologic agents** such as TNF inhibitors, including infliximab and adalimumab, to block inflammatory mediators

Early use of **DMARDs** is especially important because persistent synovitis can otherwise continue to drive structural damage.

## Extra-articular significance

RA is not limited to the joints. Because the inflammatory process can extend beyond the synovium, patients may also develop extra-articular involvement such as:

- vasculitis
- pulmonary fibrosis
- conjunctivitis
- pericarditis
- lymphadenitis

This broader involvement supports the idea of RA as a systemic inflammatory disorder.

## Clinical importance

Synovitis is important because it:

- explains swelling, warmth, pain, and tenderness in affected joints
- is associated with prolonged morning stiffness
- marks active inflammatory disease
- is linked to elevated ESR and CRP
- predicts risk of progressive joint damage if untreated
- serves as a key target in treatment of [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]]
- helps distinguish RA from [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] when combined with age, joint pattern, and lab findings
