# JOINS vs Diclofenac in Knee Osteoarthritis RCT

## Concept Overview
This concept refers to a randomized controlled trial ([[concepts/randomized-controlled-trials]]) comparing **JOINS®** with **diclofenac** for the treatment of **knee osteoarthritis**. The study examined whether JOINS could provide similar symptom relief while offering advantages in safety and tolerability.

Related sources: [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0024]], [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0025]]

## Core Clinical Question
In patients with knee osteoarthritis, can **JOINS 200 mg three times daily** achieve clinical outcomes comparable to **diclofenac SR 100 mg once daily**, with potentially better [[concepts/safety-and-tolerability|safety and tolerability]]?

## Study Snapshot
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial
- **Population:** Knee osteoarthritis patients, **n = 249**
- **Intervention:** JOINS 200 mg tid
- **Comparator:** Diclofenac SR 100 mg qd
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Primary endpoint:** VAS pain score
- **Secondary endpoints:** Lequesne index, patient global satisfaction, investigator global satisfaction

## Key Findings
- **Efficacy:** JOINS showed **similar clinical efficacy** to diclofenac over the 4-week treatment period.
- **Pain reduction:** In the VAS analysis, both groups improved, with **no statistically significant between-group difference**.
  - JOINS group: **-14.18 ± 17.53 mm**
  - Diclofenac group: **-15.49 ± 15.37 mm**
  - Between-group comparison: **p = 0.53**
- **Safety:** JOINS demonstrated **better safety** than diclofenac.
- **Tolerability:** JOINS was reported to have **superior tolerability**.

## Interpretation
The trial suggests that JOINS may be a viable treatment option for symptomatic management of knee osteoarthritis, especially when short-term pain control is needed but improved safety or tolerability is preferred over a traditional NSAID such as diclofenac.

The VAS result strengthens the comparative effectiveness claim: JOINS and diclofenac both reduced pain to a similar degree, supporting the interpretation that JOINS can function as an alternative analgesic option in OA management, not just a safety-focused adjunct.

## Why This Concept Matters
This study is useful because it connects three important evidence themes:
- **comparative effectiveness** between an alternative osteoarthritis therapy and a standard NSAID,
- **patient-centered outcomes** such as pain and global satisfaction,
- and **treatment choice considerations** where safety/tolerability may influence prescribing.
