# COX/LOX Inhibition

## Definition
COX/LOX inhibition refers to suppressing the activity of **cyclooxygenase (COX)** and **lipoxygenase (LOX)** enzymes in the **[[concepts/arachidonic-acid-pathway]]**. These enzymes help convert arachidonic acid into inflammatory mediators, so inhibiting them can reduce inflammation and pain.

## Biological role
- **COX enzymes** produce **prostaglandins**, which are strongly associated with inflammation, pain, and fever.
- **LOX enzymes** produce **leukotrienes** and related lipid mediators that also contribute to inflammatory responses.
- Because both pathways originate from arachidonic acid metabolism, dual inhibition can broaden anti-inflammatory effects.

## Mechanism in the source document
In [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0017]], Joins is described as exerting anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects by:
- inhibiting **COX-2 expression**
- suppressing **5-LOX activity**

This suggests a **dual-pathway inhibition** strategy within the arachidonic acid cascade, reducing the formation of inflammatory mediators and thereby lowering **inflammation** and **pain**.

## Key concepts
- **COX-2 inhibition**: lowers prostaglandin production
- **5-LOX inhibition**: reduces LOX-derived inflammatory signaling
- **Dual inhibition**: targeting both COX and LOX may provide broader suppression of eicosanoid-mediated inflammation
- **Arachidonic acid pathway**: the upstream metabolic route affected by COX/LOX inhibition

## Why it matters
COX/LOX inhibition is important because it targets two major branches of inflammatory mediator synthesis. In drug development, this approach is often discussed as a way to achieve anti-inflammatory efficacy while modulating multiple downstream signals involved in pain and swelling.
