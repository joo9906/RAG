# VAS Pain Assessment

## Definition
The **Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)** is a simple, patient-reported tool used to measure subjective pain intensity in clinical studies. It is typically presented as a **0–100 mm line**, where higher values indicate greater pain.

## Why it is used
VAS is widely used in clinical trials because it:
- captures patient-reported pain quickly and directly
- allows comparison of pain changes over time
- is sensitive to treatment effects in conditions such as [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] and [[rheumatoid arthritis]]

## How it works
Patients mark their pain level on a continuous line between:
- **0 mm** = no pain
- **100 mm** = worst imaginable pain

The distance from the zero point is recorded as the pain score.

## Use in clinical trials
VAS is often used as a **primary efficacy endpoint** in analgesic and anti-inflammatory trials, including studies comparing JOINS with active comparators.

### Example from osteoarthritis
In [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0025]], VAS was used as the main efficacy endpoint in an OA phase 3 trial comparing [[concepts/diclofenac]] with JOINS.

#### Reported result
- **JOINS group**: VAS change = **-14.18 ± 17.53 mm**
- **Diclofenac group**: VAS change = **-15.49 ± 15.37 mm**
- **Between-group comparison**: **p = 0.53**

This indicates that both groups experienced pain reduction, and the difference between treatments was not statistically significant.

### Example from rheumatoid arthritis
In [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0027]], VAS was the **primary endpoint** in a 6-week RCT comparing [[JOINS]] with [[celecoxib]] in patients with [[rheumatoid arthritis]]. The trial found that JOINS was **noninferior** to celecoxib for pain relief, with similar tolerability.

In a related RA phase 3 trial summarized in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0028]], both [[JOINS]] and celecoxib produced significant within-group VAS reductions:
- **JOINS group (n=87)**: Week 3 **-13.64 ± 16.62 mm**, Week 6 **-18.38 ± 20.80 mm**
- **Celecoxib group (n=87)**: Week 3 **-14.45 ± 15.89 mm**, Week 6 **-17.87 ± 19.06 mm**
- **Week 6 between-group comparison**: **p = 0.8662**

This supports the interpretation that JOINS had **analgesic efficacy comparable to celecoxib** in RA as measured by VAS.

## Key interpretation
VAS is useful for detecting treatment-related pain improvement, but results should be interpreted alongside clinical context, variability, and statistical testing. In comparative analgesic trials, similar VAS reductions across groups may indicate comparable pain-relief efficacy.
