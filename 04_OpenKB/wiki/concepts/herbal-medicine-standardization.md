# Herbal Medicine Standardization

## Overview
Herbal medicine standardization is the practice of making a plant-based medicine consistent in **composition, quality, and expected performance** across production batches. Because herbal products contain many naturally occurring compounds, standardization helps ensure that each batch has a reproducible chemical profile and remains suitable for quality control.

This concept is illustrated in two Joins Tablet ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0016]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0019]]), which describe **조인스정 (Joins Tablet)** as a prescription herbal medicine made from multiple botanical extracts and controlled through defined quality markers.

## Core idea
Unlike single-ingredient synthetic drugs, herbal medicines often rely on:
- multiple botanical sources,
- complex mixtures of constituents,
- and batch-to-batch variability caused by raw material differences.

Standardization addresses this by identifying **representative chemical markers** and using them to monitor product quality.

## Key mechanism: marker compounds
A **marker compound** is a chemically identified constituent in the raw material that is selected for **quality management** purposes. It does not always mean the compound is the sole active ingredient; rather, it serves as a reliable indicator of:
- identity,
- consistency,
- and manufacturing control.

In the Joins example, the marker compounds are:
- **oleanolic acid**
- **rosmarinic acid**

These are used to maintain consistent quality and efficacy in the finished product.

See also [[concepts/marker-compound]].

## Why standardization matters
Standardization is important because it helps herbal medicines achieve:
- **batch consistency** — same product profile across lots,
- **quality assurance** — measurable control points for manufacturing,
- **reproducibility** — more predictable clinical or functional behavior,
- **regulatory support** — clearer specification of product identity and quality.

## Example from the source documents
The source documents describe Joins as a **herbal combination drug** made from extracts of:
- *Clematis mandsurica* (위령선),
- *Trichosanthes kirilowii* (괄루근),
- *Prunella vulgaris* (하고초).

The earlier document emphasizes the use of **oleanolic acid** and **rosmarinic acid** as marker compounds, while the newer approval-information document adds practical formulation details:
- **Prescription drug** classification,
- **200 mg** of 30% ethanol dried extract (40→1),
- **MFDS class 114** ([[concepts/analgesics-anti-inflammatory-drugs]]),
- **film-coated tablet** dosage form,
- and indication for **osteoarthritis** and **rheumatoid arthritis** symptom relief.

Together, these documents show standardization at two levels:
1. **chemical standardization** through marker compounds, and
2. **regulatory/product standardization** through defined dosage, classification, and approved indications.

## Safety and clinical handling context
The newer document also lists groups requiring caution ([[concepts/medication-precautions]]):
- patients with infection or suspected infection,
- pregnant women,
- women who may be pregnant,
- and nursing mothers.

This indicates that herbal medicine standardization does not only concern manufacturing quality; it also supports safer clinical use by providing a more predictable product profile.
