# Joint Symptoms and Functional Limitations

## Definition
Joint symptoms and functional limitations refer to the common clinical signs that indicate joint disease and the ways those symptoms reduce movement, comfort, and daily function. They appear in both degenerative and inflammatory conditions, including [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] and [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]].

## Core symptom pattern
The source documents describe a shared pattern of joint-related symptoms that includes:

- **Joint pain**: discomfort in the affected joint; in osteoarthritis, pain is often activity-related, while in inflammatory disease it may reflect active synovial inflammation.
- **Morning stiffness**: stiffness on waking or after inactivity, especially prominent in inflammatory disease.
- **Reduced range of motion**: the joint cannot move through its normal arc.
- **Joint swelling**: visible or palpable enlargement of the joint.
- **Joint sounds**: cracking, grinding, or other audible joint noises may be present.
- **Joint deformity**: structural change or misalignment may develop over time.
- **Progressive joint damage**: persistent inflammation or degeneration can lead to worsening function.

These findings are summarized in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0007]], [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0011]], and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0012]].

## Clinical measurement and assessment
Joint symptoms are often captured using standardized outcome measures in clinical studies. The added source document highlights several key instruments and imaging-based evaluations used in osteoarthritis and related joint research:

- **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**: a patient-reported pain scale ranging from 0 mm (no pain) to 100 mm (worst imaginable pain).
- **K-WOMAC (Korean Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index)**: a self-report tool that measures pain, stiffness, and physical function in osteoarthritis; it includes 24 items.
- **WORMS (Whole-Organ Magnetic Resonance Imaging Score)**: a whole-knee MRI scoring system used to quantify structural deterioration.
- **Subarticular BME**: a WORMS-based score for subarticular bone marrow abnormality, which increases with osteoarthritis structural worsening.
- **dGEMRIC (delayed gadolinium-enhanced MRI of cartilage)**: an MRI technique used to assess cartilage GAG content.
- **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**: the imaging modality underlying WORMS, dGEMRIC, and other joint assessments.

The new source also extends this measurement framework with epidemiologic definitions of disease burden, showing that joint symptoms can be separated from radiographic disease and combined into clinically meaningful categories:

- **Sx**: symptomatic only
- **OA**: radiographic features only
- **Sx+OA**: both symptoms and radiographic features present

These measures show that joint symptoms and functional limitation are often evaluated alongside structural and compositional markers, not just by physical complaint alone. The document also clarifies **GAG (glycosaminoglycan)** as a key cartilage component measured indirectly by dGEMRIC.

## Functional impact
These symptoms lead to functional limitations such as:

- difficulty walking, bending, or climbing stairs
- reduced ability to use the affected limb or joint
- discomfort during routine activities
- limited joint mobility in everyday tasks
- progressive impairment when symptoms persist

In inflammatory disease such as [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis (RA)]], the mechanism is often more destructive: ongoing synovitis can progress to cartilage destruction, joint fusion, and stiffness. The formation of pannus—thickened synovial tissue that invades cartilage and bone—helps explain why function may worsen over time.

## Clinical interpretation
This concept captures the practical consequences of joint disease rather than a single diagnosis. Two broad patterns are especially useful:

- **Mechanical pattern**: symptoms, especially pain, often worsen with activity and improve with rest; short-duration morning stiffness is common in osteoarthritis.
- **Inflammatory pattern**: persistent swelling, stiffness, and reduced motion may reflect active synovial inflammation, as seen in [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]].

Recognizing which pattern is present can help distinguish degenerative joint disease from inflammatory autoimmune disease.

## Pathophysiologic links
Joint function becomes limited through different mechanisms depending on the underlying disorder:

- In [[concepts/osteoarthritis]], wear-related structural change contributes to pain, crepitus, deformity, and motion loss.
- In [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]], immune-mediated synovitis and pannus formation drive cartilage and bone erosion, leading to swelling, stiffness, deformity, and joint destruction.
- Imaging-based markers such as WORMS, subarticular BME, and dGEMRIC help connect symptoms with structural or compositional changes in the joint.
- The prevalence studies in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0040]] show that symptom burden and radiographic OA do not always align, especially when comparing sex- and joint-specific patterns in the spine, knee, hand, hip, and shoulder.
- RA may also begin with systemic symptoms in a subset of patients, including fever, fatigue, appetite loss, weight loss, depression, and anxiety, before prominent joint findings appear.

## Population burden and joint-specific patterns
The new Joins document adds epidemiologic context by showing how common symptomatic and radiographic OA can be in older adults:

- In adults aged 50 years or older, **symptomatic spinal OA** was found in **5.6% of men** and **16.0% of women**.
- In adults aged 65 years or older, radiographic OA was most common in the **spine**, followed by the **hand** and **knee**.
- Women generally showed higher rates of **symptomatic** and **combined symptomatic+radiographic** disease, particularly at the knee and hand.

This reinforces that joint symptoms and functional limitations are not uniform across the body. Their impact depends on the affected site, the balance between structural and symptomatic disease, and sex-specific patterns of burden. This perspective is closely related to [[concepts/joint-specific osteoarthritis]] and [[concepts/sex differences in disease prevalence]].

## Summary
Joint symptoms and functional limitations describe the practical consequences of joint disease: pain, stiffness, swelling, sounds, deformity, and reduced mobility. In the source documents, these features appear in osteoarthritis as a mechanical symptom pattern and in rheumatoid arthritis as the result of chronic inflammatory synovitis, morning stiffness, and pannus-driven joint damage, sometimes preceded by systemic prodromal symptoms. The added Joins document also shows how these complaints are measured in practice using tools such as VAS, K-WOMAC, WORMS, subarticular BME, and dGEMRIC, while also framing OA burden using symptomatic, radiographic, and combined disease categories across different joints and sexes.
