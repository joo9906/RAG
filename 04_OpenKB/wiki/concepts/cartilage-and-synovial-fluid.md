# Cartilage and Synovial Fluid

Cartilage and synovial fluid are two core structures that help joints move smoothly and resist damage. Cartilage provides a protective, elastic surface at the ends of bones, while synovial fluid reduces friction across that surface. Together, they are central to [[concepts/joint anatomy]] and help explain joint wear conditions such as [[concepts/osteoarthritis]].

## Cartilage

Cartilage is a flexible connective tissue that covers the ends of bones inside a joint.

### Main functions
- Protects the ends of bones
- Cushions the joint surface
- Helps absorb mechanical stress
- Supports smooth joint movement

### Key characteristics
- It is structurally similar to other supportive connective tissues
- It has elasticity, which helps protect bone ends during movement
- It does not contain blood vessels or nerves

### Internal composition
Cartilage function depends on three major components:
- [[concepts/collagen]]
- [[concepts/proteoglycan]]
- [[concepts/chondrocyte]]

#### Collagen
- Especially important in articular cartilage is **Type II collagen**
- A fibrous protein found in connective tissue such as skin, ligaments, and bone
- Provides **elasticity** and structural support

#### Proteoglycan
- The main matrix substance in cartilage
- Built from a core protein with many glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) attached
- Examples of GAGs include keratan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate
- Works with collagen to form a mesh-like tissue that helps maintain **mobility** and reduces friction

#### Chondrocyte
- The cartilage cell
- Responsible for the **growth and maintenance** of cartilage

### Why it matters
Healthy cartilage reduces direct bone-to-bone contact and helps the joint absorb stress during movement. Damage or wear in cartilage is a major factor in joint problems such as osteoarthritis.

## Synovial Fluid

Synovial fluid is the liquid found in the joint cavity.

### Main functions
- Lubricates the joint
- Reduces friction between moving surfaces
- Supports fluid, low-resistance joint motion

### Why it matters
Synovial fluid helps the joint move with less resistance and helps protect cartilage from mechanical stress. It works as part of the joint environment that supports efficient movement.

## Relationship Between the Two

Cartilage and synovial fluid work together to maintain joint function:
- Cartilage provides a protective surface at the ends of bones
- Its collagen-proteoglycan matrix gives the tissue elasticity and mobility
- Chondrocytes maintain the cartilage over time
- Synovial fluid reduces friction across the cartilage surface
- Together, they help joints move efficiently and stay protected

This relationship becomes clearer when viewed alongside the other main joint structures described in the source documents: [[concepts/synovial membrane]], [[concepts/ligament]], and the [[concepts/joint cavity]]. The synovial membrane produces synovial fluid, while ligaments help stabilize the joint.

## Relation to Osteoarthritis

The added source on [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] describes OA as the most common form of arthritis and as a disease in which joint cartilage wears down, exposing the underlying bone and triggering inflammation in the surrounding synovial membrane. This reinforces the role of cartilage and synovial fluid as protective components of joint health:
- Cartilage loss exposes bone and contributes to pain and deformity
- Synovial inflammation can accompany cartilage degeneration
- Reduced protection and lubrication increase mechanical stress on the joint

## Source Context

The source documents explain that joints are made up of structures such as cartilage, ligaments, synovial membrane, synovial fluid, and the joint cavity. One document focuses specifically on cartilage, emphasizing its elasticity and lack of blood vessels and nerves, while another introduces cartilage and synovial fluid together as essential parts of joint anatomy. Combined with the newer source on osteoarthritis, these documents show that cartilage depends on collagen, proteoglycan, and chondrocytes to maintain its structure and function. Synovial fluid complements this by reducing friction and supporting smooth movement, forming a basic foundation for understanding joint health and osteoarthritis.
