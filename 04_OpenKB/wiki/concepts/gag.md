# Glycosaminoglycan (GAG)

## Definition
Glycosaminoglycan (GAG) is a long-chain sugar molecule that forms part of the cartilage extracellular matrix. In cartilage, GAG is a key component of proteoglycans and helps maintain the tissue’s structure, hydration, and mechanical resilience.

## Why it matters
GAG content is commonly used as an indicator of cartilage health. When cartilage loses GAG, it often reflects degeneration or reduced matrix integrity, which is important in conditions such as [[concepts/osteoarthritis]].

## Relation to imaging and assessment
In the source document, GAG content is evaluated indirectly using the [[concepts/dgemric]] index. The dGEMRIC method measures cartilage properties related to GAG concentration, making it useful for tracking whether a treatment preserves cartilage composition.

## Evidence from the source document
The summary [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0031]] reports that:
- JOINS® maintained the dGEMRIC index over 1 year in the treatment group.
- The placebo group showed a significant decline in dGEMRIC index.
- This difference suggests preservation of cartilage GAG content in the JOINS® group.

## Related concepts
- [[concepts/dgemric]] — imaging-based index used to estimate cartilage GAG content
- [[concepts/cartilage-protection]] — preservation of cartilage matrix components such as GAG
- [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] — disease context in which GAG loss is clinically relevant

## In context
GAG is not only a structural component of cartilage but also a biomarker-like target for evaluating whether a therapy may help slow cartilage degeneration. In this document, maintained GAG-related imaging results are presented as evidence of cartilage protection.
