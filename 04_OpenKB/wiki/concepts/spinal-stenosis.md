# Spinal Stenosis

## Overview
Spinal stenosis is a condition in which the **spinal canal becomes narrowed**, reducing space for the **spinal cord** and/or **spinal nerve roots**. When this narrowing leads to compression, it can cause neurologic symptoms such as pain, numbness, tingling, and weakness.

In the source document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0041]], spinal stenosis is presented as a possible consequence of [[concepts/spondylosis]], or spinal osteoarthritis.

## Relationship to Spondylosis
[[concepts/spondylosis]] refers to degenerative changes in the spine. As these changes progress, bone spurs, disc degeneration, and joint thickening may contribute to narrowing of the spinal canal. This makes spinal stenosis a common downstream complication of degenerative spinal disease.

## Key Features
- **Anatomy involved**: spinal canal, spinal cord, spinal nerve roots
- **Core problem**: narrowing of available space
- **Main effect**: compression of neural structures
- **Common symptoms**:
  - pain
  - numbness
  - tingling
  - muscle weakness

## Clinical Significance
The importance of spinal stenosis is that symptoms may reflect not only local back discomfort but also **nerve compression**. This helps explain why patients with degenerative spinal conditions may experience sensory changes or weakness in addition to pain and stiffness.

## Diagnostic Evaluation
The source document notes that imaging is used to assess spinal disease and nerve involvement:
- **X-ray**: to evaluate arthritis or bony change
- **MRI**: to visualize the spinal cord and surrounding nerves, especially for compression
- **CT**: to examine the spinal canal, bones, and joints

Among these, MRI is especially useful when spinal stenosis is suspected because it can show narrowing and neural compression more directly.

## Management Context
Spinal stenosis is typically managed according to the underlying cause and symptom severity. In the source document, treatment of degenerative spinal disease includes:
- exercise
- weight loss
- massage
- heat therapy
- analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs
- injections
- surgery in more severe cases

This places spinal stenosis within the broader framework of [[concepts/pain-management]] and degenerative musculoskeletal disease.
