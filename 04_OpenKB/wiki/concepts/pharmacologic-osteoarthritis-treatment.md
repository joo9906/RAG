# Medication-Based OA Care Using NSAIDs, Intra-Articular Steroids, and Symptom-Relief Agents

## Overview
Medication-based osteoarthritis (OA) care focuses on **symptom control**: reducing pain, improving function, and supporting quality of life. Across the source documents, OA pharmacotherapy is presented as a **multimodal management strategy** rather than a curative approach. The 2019 ACR guideline summary in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0010]] emphasizes guideline-based options for hand, knee, and hip OA, while later sources add a mechanism-focused anti-inflammatory example ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0017]]), an approved prescription symptom-relief product ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0019|Joins Tablet]]), post-marketing safety data ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]]), and randomized trial evidence showing comparable short-term efficacy versus diclofenac with better tolerability and equivalent pain reduction on VAS ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0024]] [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0025]]). A newer phase 4 study in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0033]] further supports long-term improvement in pain and joint function with JOINS® versus placebo. The spinal osteoarthritis source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0041]] broadens this framing by showing that OA-like degenerative spine disease is also managed through pain relief and functional improvement, with Joins considered potentially applicable under its approved labeling.

## Core Treatment Options

### NSAIDs
Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are a central pharmacologic option in OA management. They are used to relieve pain and reduce inflammatory symptoms, especially when nonpharmacologic measures alone are insufficient. NSAIDs are a guideline-supported component of OA care and appear prominently in the ACR recommendations summarized in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0010]].

### Intra-articular steroid injections
Intra-articular (IA) steroid injections are another important intervention. They are used for targeted symptom relief in affected joints, particularly when pain is localized or when conservative therapy has not provided enough benefit. The source document highlights IA steroid injections as part of the OA treatment framework.

### Symptom-relief agents with anti-inflammatory activity
The Joins Tablet source ([[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0019]]) provides an example of a prescription medication used for symptom relief in OA. It is an extract-based tablet containing **willow vine, Trichosanthes root, and Prunella herb** extract, approved for relief of symptoms in **osteoarthritis** and **rheumatoid arthritis**.

Key product details include:
- **Route and dose:** 1 tablet orally, 3 times daily
- **Formulation:** oval yellow-brown film-coated tablet
- **Therapeutic class:** [[concepts/anti-inflammatory-mechanisms|antipyretic, analgesic, anti-inflammatory agent]] classification under MFDS category 114
- **Clinical role:** symptom control rather than disease cure

## Mechanistic Extension: Anti-inflammatory Pathway Modulation
The source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0017]] describes Joins as an agent with anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity through the **[[concepts/arachidonic-acid-pathway|arachidonic acid pathway]]**. Its reported actions include:

- inhibition of **COX-2 expression**
- suppression of **5-LOX activity**
- reduced production of **prostaglandins** and related inflammatory mediators

This adds a broader pharmacologic perspective to OA symptom control: beyond conventional NSAIDs and injections, some therapies are framed by their ability to interfere with eicosanoid-mediated inflammation through **[[concepts/cox-lox-inhibition|COX/LOX inhibition]]**.

## Clinical Evidence for Symptom Relief Therapy
The randomized clinical evidence summarized in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0024]] supports Joins for knee OA. In a **4-week RCT** involving **249 patients**, **JOINS 200 mg three times daily** was compared with **diclofenac SR 100 mg once daily**.

### Endpoints
- **Primary endpoint:** VAS
- **Secondary endpoints:** Lequesne index, patients’ global satisfaction score, investigators’ global satisfaction score

### Conclusion
- JOINS showed **similar clinical efficacy** to diclofenac over 4 weeks.
- JOINS had **better safety and tolerability** than diclofenac.

The follow-up summary in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0025]] adds a focused comparison of **VAS pain score change** in an OA phase 3 setting. At 4 weeks, both groups improved, with **JOINS at -14.18 ± 17.53 mm** and **diclofenac at -15.49 ± 15.37 mm**, and no significant between-group difference (**p = 0.53**). This reinforces the interpretation that JOINS can provide **comparable analgesic benefit** to diclofenac in OA.

The newer phase 4 study in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0033]] extends this evidence base into longer-term treatment. In a **1-year** OA cartilage-protection clinical trial, JOINS® showed **significant improvement versus placebo** in both:
- **VAS** pain scores
- **K-WOMAC** scores, reflecting pain, stiffness, and physical function

Together, these studies strengthen the role of symptom-relief agents in OA care by showing that an approved product can provide both **short-term pain relief** and **longer-term functional benefit**, with a tolerability profile that may be more favorable than standard NSAID options.

## Safety and Tolerability Considerations
The post-marketing surveillance source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]] provides practical safety information for Joins. In a Korean post-marketing study of **5,962 patients** treated for osteoarthritis/degenerative joint disease, adverse events were reported in **3.02%** of patients overall, and events that could not be ruled out as drug-related occurred in **2.38%**.

The most frequently observed adverse reactions were gastrointestinal, including:
- heartburn
- dyspepsia
- epigastric discomfort
- gastrointestinal disorder
- epigastric pain

Other reported reactions included edema, headache, pruritus, rash, leukopenia, insomnia, palpitations, AST/ALT elevation, and dizziness. This reinforces that OA symptom-relief drugs should be selected with attention to **[[concepts/medication-precautions]]**, especially when gastrointestinal tolerability is a concern.

## Guideline Context
The 2019 ACR/Arthritis Foundation guideline organizes OA therapies by recommendation strength, including **strongly recommended** and **conditionally recommended** options. Pharmacologic treatment is presented alongside nonpharmacologic care such as exercise and weight loss, reflecting the idea that OA management is usually multimodal rather than medication-only.

Medication choice in OA is generally guided by:
- joint location and symptom pattern
- severity of pain and functional limitation
- response to nonpharmacologic measures
- safety considerations and patient comorbidities
- whether treatment is aimed at localized flares, chronic symptom control, or broader inflammation-related relief

## Clinical Role
Medication-based OA care is typically used to:
- reduce pain
- improve daily function
- support mobility and quality of life
- provide symptom control when lifestyle measures are not enough

Because OA is a chronic degenerative condition, these therapies are generally aimed at **symptom management** rather than cure.

The new spinal osteoarthritis source [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0041]] extends this clinical role beyond peripheral joint OA. It describes spondylosis as a form of **spinal osteoarthritis** with degenerative changes in the spine, often managed by pain relief and functional improvement. Standard options include NSAIDs, topical agents, mild narcotics, steroid injections, exercise, weight loss, massage, and heat therapy. The document notes that **Joins® has no direct clinical study in spondylosis**, but may still be used in spinal osteoarthritis according to its approved indications, with expectation of pain reduction and possible structural benefit based on OA/RA evidence.

## Practical Interpretation of the Sources
Taken together, the source documents suggest a layered view of OA pharmacotherapy:

1. **Guideline-based symptom control** with NSAIDs and targeted IA steroid injections [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0010]]
2. **Mechanism-based anti-inflammatory support** through modulation of COX/LOX-related pathways [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0017]]
3. **Approved prescription symptom-relief options** such as Joins Tablet, which is indicated for OA symptom relief and has a post-marketing safety profile dominated by gastrointestinal adverse reactions [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0019]] [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]]
4. **Clinical trial support for comparative efficacy and tolerability**, showing Joins can perform similarly to diclofenac in short-term knee OA treatment with better safety and tolerability, including non-inferior VAS pain reduction in OA phase 3 data [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0024]] [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0025]]
5. **Longer-term functional improvement**, with 1-year JOINS® therapy improving both pain and K-WOMAC outcomes versus placebo in OA cartilage-protection phase 4 evidence [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0033]]
6. **Extension to degenerative spine disease**, where symptom-relief therapy remains relevant for spinal osteoarthritis and Joins may be considered within labeled use despite the absence of direct spondylosis-specific trials [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0041]]
