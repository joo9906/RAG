# Osteoarthritis Treatment Classification

## Overview
Osteoarthritis (OA) treatment can be organized by **what the therapy targets**, **how quickly it acts**, and **whether it is intended to relieve symptoms or modify disease progression**. In the source materials, therapies are grouped into:

- **Non-pharmacologic treatment**
- **Symptomatic rapid-acting drugs**
- **Slow-acting symptomatic agents**
- **Potential disease-modifying agents**

This framework helps distinguish therapies that mainly relieve symptoms from those intended to alter the course of disease, as discussed in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0009]]. A related source, [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0010]], adds a guideline-based view of OA care from the 2019 ACR recommendations, emphasizing how treatments are prioritized as **strongly recommended** or **conditionally recommended**.

A later source, [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0030]], adds evidence for a slow-acting symptomatic agent, **JOINS®**, which was evaluated in a randomized controlled trial using MRI-based structural outcomes. That study compared JOINS with placebo in knee OA and found superior **cartilage-protective** effects, reinforcing JOINS as a representative [[concepts/disease-modifying-osteoarthritis-drugs|potential disease-modifying]] or structure-preserving OA therapy candidate.

## Core Treatment Goals in OA
OA treatment is generally aimed at:

- reducing pain
- maintaining joint function
- preventing deformity
- improving quality of life

These goals are central to [[concepts/osteoarthritis-management]] and explain why OA therapy is usually **long-term, multimodal, and individualized**.

## Main Classification of OA Treatments

### 1) Non-Pharmacologic Therapy
This is a foundational part of OA care and includes:

- exercise
- weight control
- muscle strengthening

The newer source reinforces this category through the 2019 ACR guideline, which gives strong emphasis to **exercise** and **weight loss** in appropriate patients. These interventions reduce mechanical stress on joints, support function, and are essential in [[concepts/osteoarthritis-management]]. They also reflect the broader role of [[concepts/nonpharmacologic-treatment]] and [[concepts/lifestyle-interventions]].

### 2) Symptomatic Rapid-Acting Drugs in OA
These medications are used for relatively fast pain relief and inflammation control.

Examples include:

- acetaminophen
- NSAIDs
- intra-articular corticosteroids

The 2019 ACR guideline summary adds practical relevance here by highlighting **NSAIDs** and **intra-articular (IA) steroid injections** as key treatment options for hand, knee, and hip OA. These treatments primarily relieve pain and inflammation rather than changing disease progression. See also [[concepts/NSAIDs]] and [[concepts/intra-articular-injection]].

### 3) Symptomatic Slow-Acting Drugs in OA (SYSADOA)
SYSADOA are therapies that act more slowly but may provide ongoing symptomatic benefit.

Examples include:

- glucosamine/chondroitin sulfate
- avocado/soybean unsaponifiables
- diacerin
- Joins®
- JOINS®

The source materials place **Joins®/JOINS®** in this category. One document describes its mechanism as **COX-2 inhibition** and **5-LOX suppression** within the **[[concepts/arachidonic-acid-pathway|arachidonic acid pathway]]**, linking it to [[concepts/anti-inflammatory-mechanisms]]. Another document extends this view by showing that JOINS was tested in an RCT with **MRI biomarkers** and demonstrated superior **cartilage-protective** effects compared with placebo. Together, these sources suggest that some SYSADOA may also be discussed as potential structure-preserving agents, blurring the line between symptom relief and disease modification.

### 4) Disease-Modifying OA Drugs (DMOADs)
DMOADs are intended to reduce or modify the structural progression of OA.

In theory, these drugs would:

- slow cartilage degeneration
- reduce structural joint damage
- change disease course rather than only symptoms

However, the source notes that **no current therapy has been proven to prevent OA-related structural progression**. This remains a key limitation in the field and is central to [[concepts/disease-modifying-osteoarthritis-drugs]]. The JOINS RCT summarized in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0030]] is notable because it contributes structural MRI evidence in this area, even if it does not establish a definitive DMOAD.

## Guideline Perspective: 2019 ACR OA Recommendations
The 2019 ACR guideline provides a clinically practical layer to this classification by separating therapies into **strongly recommended** and **conditionally recommended** groups for **hand, knee, and hip OA**.

Common examples highlighted in the source include:

- **Exercise**
- **Weight loss**
- **NSAIDs**
- **Intra-articular steroids**

This perspective shows that OA treatment classification is not only about drug mechanism, but also about **clinical strength of recommendation**. It connects this concept to broader evidence-based decision-making in [[concepts/rheumatology-guidelines]] and [[concepts/osteoarthritis-management]].

## Important Clinical Distinction
A major concept in OA treatment classification is the difference between:

- **symptom control** and
- **disease modification**

Most currently available therapies are symptomatic. Even when they are effective, they do not clearly alter the underlying degenerative process. The guideline-based material emphasizes this practical reality by focusing on treatments that improve pain and function, such as exercise, weight loss, NSAIDs, and injections, rather than presenting established disease-modifying therapies.

## Practical Interpretation
This classification can be understood as a continuum:

- **Non-pharmacologic care** supports the joint and reduces stress
- **Rapid-acting symptomatic drugs** provide quick relief
- **SYSADOA** provide slower symptomatic benefit
- **DMOADs** represent the goal of altering disease progression
- **Guideline-based care** prioritizes treatments by evidence strength, as in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0010]]

In practice, OA care usually combines multiple approaches rather than relying on a single treatment class.
