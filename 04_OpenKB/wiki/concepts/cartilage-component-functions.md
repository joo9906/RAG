# Functions of Cartilage Components that Determine Elasticity, Mobility, and Maintenance

[[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0003]] and [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0004]] show that cartilage function depends on both its structural components and the biochemical balance that preserves the matrix. [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]] adds that this balance is also shaped by oxidative stress, inflammatory signaling, and chondrocyte survival.

## Overview
Cartilage is a connective tissue built from a collagen framework, a proteoglycan-rich matrix, and resident cartilage cells. Its functional properties are determined by how these components interact and how matrix synthesis is balanced against degradation.

The main factors are:

- **[[concepts/collagen]]** provides structural support and elasticity
- **[[concepts/proteoglycan]]** provides hydration, mobility, and friction reduction
- **[[concepts/chondrocyte]]** supports growth and maintenance of cartilage tissue
- **MMPs, TIMPs, cytokines, free radicals, and apoptosis-related pathways** regulate cartilage breakdown and preservation

## Key components and functions

### Collagen
- The main collagen in articular cartilage is **Type II collagen**.
- Collagen is a fibrous protein found in connective tissues such as skin, ligaments, and bone.
- In cartilage, collagen forms a scaffold that provides **mechanical strength** and contributes to **elasticity**.
- This framework helps cartilage resist deformation while maintaining shape.

### Proteoglycan
- Proteoglycans are the major substances forming the cartilage matrix.
- They consist of a core protein with multiple **glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)** attached.
- Examples of GAGs include **keratan sulfate** and **chondroitin sulfate**.
- Proteoglycans interact with collagen to form a mesh-like structure that helps cartilage retain **hydration**, supports **mobility**, and reduces **friction** in joints.
- Because of their water-binding capacity, they are essential for shock absorption and smooth joint movement.

### Chondrocyte
- Chondrocytes are the cells of cartilage.
- Their role is to support the **growth** and **maintenance** of cartilage.
- They synthesize and regulate matrix components, helping preserve tissue integrity over time.
- Chondrocytes are also involved in responding to injury and maintaining the balance between matrix formation and breakdown.
- Preserving chondrocyte viability is important because chondrocyte loss through apoptosis can weaken cartilage maintenance.

## Biochemical maintenance of cartilage
Cartilage maintenance depends not only on its structural matrix but also on the balance between synthesis and degradation.

### MMPs
- **MMPs (matrix metalloproteinases)** are the primary enzymes responsible for cartilage degradation.
- Examples include:
  - **MMP-1**: collagenase
  - **MMP-2**: gelatinase
  - **MMP-3**: stromelysin
- MMPs break down the extracellular matrix and can contribute to cartilage damage when overactive.
- Their activity is promoted by inflammatory mediators such as **[[concepts/inflammatory cytokines]]** and **[[concepts/IL-1 signaling]]**.

### TIMPs
- **TIMPs (tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases)** inhibit MMP activity.
- They serve as a protective counterbalance to matrix degradation.
- A proper **MMP–TIMP balance** is necessary for cartilage homeostasis.

### Cytokines
- **IL-1** and **TNF-α** are key cytokines mentioned in the document set.
- These inflammatory signals support the action of MMPs and promote cartilage breakdown.
- When cytokine activity increases, matrix degradation can outpace repair.

### Oxidative stress and free radicals
- Free radicals are highly reactive molecules with a very short half-life.
- When they act on cartilage tissue, they contribute to arthritis development and are described as an important pathogenic factor.
- Oxidative stress can amplify inflammatory damage and accelerate matrix deterioration.

### Chondrocyte apoptosis
- Chondrocyte apoptosis refers to programmed cell death of cartilage cells.
- Because chondrocytes are essential for producing and maintaining cartilage, apoptosis reduces the tissue’s ability to repair itself.
- Preventing chondrocyte apoptosis helps preserve cartilage structure and function.

## Functional relationship
Cartilage function is determined by the interaction of its matrix components and its biochemical regulators:

- **Collagen** forms the supporting scaffold and contributes to elasticity
- **Proteoglycan** fills the matrix, binds water, and supports mobility
- **Chondrocyte** maintains and repairs the tissue
- **MMPs**, **TIMPs**, **cytokines**, **free radicals**, and **apoptosis pathways** regulate whether the matrix is preserved or degraded

When this system is balanced, cartilage remains resilient, smooth, and functional under mechanical stress. When the balance is disrupted, especially through excessive MMP activity, inflammatory cytokine signaling, oxidative damage, and chondrocyte loss, cartilage tissue is damaged.

## Relevance to cartilage-protective agents
The Joins document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]] frames cartilage protection as a multi-target process involving:

- **Free radical inhibition**
- **IL-1 inhibition**
- **MMP inhibition**
- **Chondrocyte apoptosis inhibition**

This supports the broader model that cartilage maintenance depends on both structural integrity and suppression of degenerative signaling.
