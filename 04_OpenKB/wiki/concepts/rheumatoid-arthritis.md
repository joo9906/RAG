# Rheumatoid Arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a **chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease** characterized by **synovitis** that primarily affects the **small joints** and often presents in a **symmetric** pattern. It can lead to progressive joint damage, functional impairment, and systemic manifestations. RA is distinguished from [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] by its inflammatory, systemic, and typically symmetric presentation.

## Core features

- **Cause / mechanism**: autoimmune inflammation with **synovitis** as the primary lesion
- **Typical age**: can occur at **any age**, often beginning in **young to middle adulthood** (around 20–40 years)
- **Joint pattern**: usually **symmetric**
- **Common joints**: **small joints** of the hands, especially the **fingers and wrists**
- **Course**: generally **rapidly progressive** compared with degenerative arthritis
- **Morning stiffness**: often **1 hour or longer**
- **Systemic involvement**: may extend beyond the joints and cause **whole-body inflammatory effects**

## Clinical and laboratory clues

RA is often suggested by a combination of inflammatory symptoms and laboratory findings:

- **RF (rheumatoid factor)**: often **positive**
- **ESR**: typically **elevated**
- **CRP**: typically **elevated**

These findings help differentiate RA from [[concepts/osteoarthritis]], which is more commonly associated with mechanical wear, short-lived stiffness, and negative serology.

## Clinical evidence in RA

RA is an important disease context in the JOINS evidence base. Multiple phase 3 studies comparing **JOINS®** with **celecoxib** show that JOINS provides clinically meaningful pain reduction with a similar tolerability profile.

### VAS outcome evidence

- **JOINS group (n=87)**
  - Week 3: **-13.64 ± 16.62 mm**
  - Week 6: **-18.38 ± 20.80 mm**
  - Within-group reduction was significant (**p < 0.001**)

- **Celecoxib group (n=87)**
  - Week 3: **-14.45 ± 15.89 mm**
  - Week 6: **-17.87 ± 19.06 mm**
  - Within-group reduction was significant (**p < 0.001**)

- **Between-group comparison at Week 6**
  - No significant difference in VAS change (**p = 0.8662**)

This supports the interpretation that **JOINS showed analgesic efficacy comparable to celecoxib** in RA patients. Related concepts include [[concepts/visual analog scale]], [[concepts/pain relief]], [[concepts/celecoxib]], [[concepts/analgesic-equivalence]], and [[concepts/tolerability]].

### Safety and adverse reaction evidence

A separate phase 3 RA analysis compared adverse events and drug-related adverse reactions between JOINS and celecoxib and found no meaningful safety difference.

- **JOINS group (n=91)**: adverse reactions in **41.8%**
- **Celecoxib group (n=92)**: adverse reactions in **39.1%**
- **p = 0.7171**

- **Drug-related adverse reactions**:
  - JOINS: **29.7%**
  - Celecoxib: **23.9%**
  - **p = 0.3792**

These findings indicate that JOINS had a **comparable tolerability profile** to celecoxib in the RA trial setting, supporting links to [[concepts/adverse reactions]], [[concepts/tolerability]], and [[concepts/NSAID safety]].

### RA indication and non-inferiority evidence

A later JOINS summary explicitly states that **JOINS® can be prescribed with reimbursement for RA**, while **Imotone®** is limited to **knee osteoarthritis**. It also summarizes a phase 3 **randomized controlled trial (RCT)** in RA patients showing that JOINS was **non-inferior to celecoxib** for pain relief and had similar tolerability.

Key trial details:
- **Study type:** RCT
- **Objective:** compare pain relief and tolerability of JOINS® vs. celecoxib in RA patients
- **Participants:** RA patients, **n = 183**
- **Intervention:** JOINS **200 mg tid** vs. celecoxib **200 mg bid**
- **Duration:** 6 weeks
- **Endpoints:**
  - **Primary:** VAS
  - **Secondary:** rescue medication use frequency, **20% improvement response rate**
- **Conclusion:** JOINS was **non-inferior** to celecoxib for pain relief, with **similar tolerability**

This strengthens the RA evidence base for JOINS and links RA to [[concepts/noninferiority]], [[concepts/rescue medication]], and [[concepts/20% improvement response rate]].

## Comparison with osteoarthritis

Compared with [[concepts/osteoarthritis]]:

- RA is **inflammatory**, while OA is **degenerative**
- RA commonly affects **small, symmetric joints**; OA often affects **weight-bearing joints** and is more **asymmetric**
- RA has **prolonged morning stiffness**; OA stiffness is usually **brief**
- RA often shows **RF positivity** and **elevated ESR/CRP**; OA usually does not

## Clinical significance

Recognizing RA early is important because it can progress quickly and lead to persistent synovial inflammation, joint destruction, and functional impairment. The combination of **prolonged morning stiffness**, **symmetric small-joint involvement**, and **inflammatory lab markers** is a key diagnostic pattern.

In the JOINS evidence base, RA is also a key therapeutic setting because the product has been studied against celecoxib in both efficacy and safety analyses, with results supporting **comparable pain relief** and **similar tolerability**. The additional summary in document 0039 further emphasizes **RA reimbursement positioning** and **non-inferiority evidence**.
