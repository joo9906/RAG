# Pregnancy and Lactation Drug Safety

## Overview
Pregnancy and lactation drug safety refers to the cautious use of medicines during pregnancy, possible pregnancy, and breastfeeding, when the potential benefit of treatment must be weighed against possible risks to the mother, fetus, or infant.

This concept is illustrated in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0021]], which states that a drug should be used in pregnancy only when the therapeutic benefit is judged to outweigh the risk, and that breastfeeding should be stopped when safety during lactation has not been established.

## Key principles

### 1. Use in pregnancy only when benefit outweighs risk
- If a patient is pregnant or may be pregnant, medication should generally be avoided unless the expected therapeutic benefit clearly exceeds the potential risk.
- This reflects a conservative risk-benefit approach, especially when human pregnancy safety data are limited.

### 2. Breastfeeding safety must be established
- When lactation safety is not confirmed, the medication should not be assumed safe for the nursing infant.
- In the source document, the instruction is to **discontinue breastfeeding** if the medicine is taken.

### 3. Pediatric experience is often limited
- Although this concept centers on pregnancy and lactation, related labeling often notes limited or no pediatric safety experience as part of broader reproductive and family-use caution.
- The source document states that safety in children has not been established and there is no use experience.

## Clinical implications
- Always verify pregnancy status before prescribing potentially risky drugs.
- Counsel patients on possible fetal and infant exposure.
- Discuss alternatives when safer options exist.
- Provide clear guidance on breastfeeding interruption when needed.
