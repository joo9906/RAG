# Subarticular Bone Marrow Edema

## Definition
Subarticular Bone Marrow Edema (BME) refers to abnormal changes in the bone marrow just beneath the joint surface. In osteoarthritis research, it is commonly assessed on MRI using the **Subarticular Bone Marrow abnormality scores for Entire Knee**.

## Why It Matters
Subarticular BME is used as a structural biomarker in [[concepts/knee osteoarthritis]]. An increase in score is associated with worsening joint structure and progression of osteoarthritic damage.

## Measurement
- Typically evaluated by **MRI**
- Scored across the **entire knee**
- Higher scores indicate greater abnormality

## Relation to the Source Document
The document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0032]] reports that in a phase 4 OA cartilage-protection study, [[concepts/JOINS]] showed a significant effect on subarticular BME over 2 years.

### Reported finding
- JOINS® maintained subarticular BME over 2 years
- Placebo showed a significant increase
- This suggests a potential structural protective effect in OA

## Interpretation in OA
Subarticular BME is important because it provides a noninvasive way to track:
- structural worsening
- subchondral bone involvement
- response to disease-modifying or cartilage-protective interventions
