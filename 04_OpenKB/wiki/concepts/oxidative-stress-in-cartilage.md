# Oxidative Stress in Cartilage

## Overview
Oxidative stress in cartilage refers to the damaging effects of **free radicals** and other reactive oxygen species on joint tissue. In degenerative joint disease, these molecules can accelerate inflammation, matrix breakdown, and cell death, contributing to cartilage loss over time.

This concept is highlighted in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]], where free radical inhibition is presented as one of the main cartilage-protective mechanisms of Joins.

## Why it matters in cartilage
Cartilage has limited regenerative capacity, so oxidative injury can have lasting consequences. When free radicals act on cartilage tissue, they can:

- trigger or worsen [[concepts/inflammation]]
- promote [[concepts/cartilage degeneration]]
- support destructive enzyme activity such as [[concepts/MMP inhibition|MMPs]]
- contribute to chondrocyte damage and [[concepts/chondrocyte apoptosis]]

## Key ideas from the source document
The source document describes free radicals as:

- highly reactive species
- short-lived molecules with strong reactivity
- an important pathogenic factor in arthritis when they affect cartilage

It presents **free radical inhibition** as part of Joins’ proposed cartilage-protective action, alongside:

- IL-1 inhibition
- MMP inhibition
- chondrocyte apoptosis inhibition

## Relationship to cartilage damage
Oxidative stress can amplify joint injury through multiple pathways:

1. **Direct tissue injury** — reactive molecules damage cartilage components
2. **Inflammatory signaling** — oxidative stress can reinforce [[concepts/inflammation]]
3. **Enzyme activation** — it can support matrix-degrading pathways such as MMPs
4. **Cell loss** — it can increase chondrocyte vulnerability and apoptosis
