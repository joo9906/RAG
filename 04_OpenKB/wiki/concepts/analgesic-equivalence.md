# Analgesic Equivalence

## Definition
Analgesic equivalence refers to a situation in which two treatments produce **similar pain-relieving effects**, with **no statistically significant difference** between them on a pain outcome measure.

## Why it matters
In clinical studies, analgesic equivalence suggests that a new or alternative therapy may provide pain control comparable to a standard treatment. This is often assessed using patient-reported pain scales such as [[concepts/vas-pain-score]].

## Example from the source document
The summary page [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0028]] describes a phase 3 rheumatoid arthritis trial comparing **JOINS®** with **celecoxib**.

Key findings:
- Both groups showed significant reductions in VAS pain scores over time.
- **JOINS group (n=87):**
  - Week 3: -13.64 ± 16.62 mm
  - Week 6: -18.38 ± 20.80 mm
- **Celecoxib group (n=87):**
  - Week 3: -14.45 ± 15.89 mm
  - Week 6: -17.87 ± 19.06 mm
- The between-group difference at Week 6 was **not significant** (**p = 0.8662**).

This is presented as evidence that **JOINS® has analgesic efficacy equivalent to celecoxib**.

## Interpretation
Analgesic equivalence does not necessarily mean two treatments are identical in every way. It means that, for the measured pain outcome and study conditions, their effects are close enough that **no meaningful difference was detected**.

Important considerations:
- Equivalence depends on the **specific outcome measure** used.
- It depends on the **population** studied, such as patients with [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]].
- It depends on the **trial design**, duration, and statistical analysis.
