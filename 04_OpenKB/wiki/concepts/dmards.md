# DMARDs

**DMARDs** stands for **disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs**. These medications are used to **reduce disease activity**, **induce remission**, or **slow the progression** of inflammatory arthritis, especially [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] / rheumatoid arthritis (RA).

## Core role

Unlike symptomatic treatments such as [[concepts/NSAIDs]] and [[concepts/corticosteroids]], DMARDs are intended to **modify the course of the disease** rather than only relieve pain or inflammation.

## Key points from the source document

The source summary on RA treatment notes that DMARDs are used to:

- induce remission in rheumatoid arthritis
- slow the rate of disease progression
- improve outcomes when started early

This aligns with the general treatment principle that **early DMARD use leads to better long-term results**.

## Examples mentioned in the source

- **Methotrexate**
- **Sulfasalazine**
- **Leflunomide**

These are commonly used conventional DMARDs in RA management.

## Relationship to other treatments

DMARDs are often part of a broader RA treatment strategy that may also include:

- [[concepts/NSAIDs]] for pain and inflammation relief
- [[concepts/corticosteroids]] for rapid anti-inflammatory control
- [[concepts/biologic-therapy]] when conventional DMARDs are insufficient or more targeted immunomodulation is needed

## Clinical significance

In RA, DMARDs are important because they help prevent:

- irreversible joint damage
- persistent inflammation
- functional decline over time

They are a central therapy in long-term disease control and are often started early to preserve joint function and quality of life.
