# Pannus

Pannus is an abnormal mass of **thickened synovial tissue** that forms in chronic inflammatory joint disease, especially [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]]. It develops when the synovium becomes hyperplastic and infiltrated by inflammatory cells, allowing the tissue to spread over and into adjacent joint structures.

## Definition

In the context of [[concepts/synovitis]], pannus refers to:

- proliferating synovial cells
- infiltrating inflammatory cells
- a thickened tissue mass that grows into surrounding structures

This tissue is not just inflamed synovium; it becomes **aggressively invasive** and contributes directly to structural joint damage.

## Role in rheumatoid arthritis

The source document [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0011]] describes pannus as a key mechanism in the progression of [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]]:

- **Inflammatory synovitis** begins in the joint lining
- The synovium proliferates and becomes inflamed
- **Pannus** forms as the tissue thickens and expands
- Pannus invades **cartilage** and **bone**
- This invasion causes **erosion**, leading to joint destruction

## Pathological significance

Pannus is important because it explains how chronic inflammation turns into irreversible damage:

- it physically侵ades cartilage
- it promotes bone and joint surface erosion
- it contributes to **joint fusion** and **stiffness** over time

In other words, pannus is a major link between immune-mediated inflammation and the visible structural damage seen in RA.
