# Post-Marketing Surveillance

## Overview
Post-marketing surveillance is the systematic monitoring of a medicine's safety after it has been approved and released for real-world use. It is used to detect adverse reactions that may not have been fully observed during pre-approval trials, especially in larger and more diverse patient populations.

This concept is illustrated in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0020]], which reports post-marketing safety findings for [[Joins tablets]] in patients with osteoarthritis / degenerative joint disease.

It is also reinforced by [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0034]], which presents a 2-year Phase 4 safety comparison showing that JOINS had a placebo-like safety profile.

## Why it matters
Clinical trials often involve limited sample sizes and controlled conditions. Post-marketing surveillance helps identify:
- uncommon or delayed adverse reactions
- reactions in broader patient groups
- frequency patterns across organ systems
- safety signals that may affect labeling or clinical use

## Key examples from the source documents

### 1. Real-world surveillance report
The Joins tablet surveillance report provides a practical example of how post-marketing safety data are summarized:
- Surveillance period: **2001.07.10–2005.07.09**
- Population: **5,962 patients**
- Overall adverse event rate: **3.02%** regardless of causality
- Drug-related adverse events: **2.38%** where causality could not be excluded

### 2. Long-term Phase 4 safety comparison
The Phase 4 OA cartilage-protection study adds controlled comparative evidence over 2 years:
- **JOINS group (n=38)**
  - Adverse events: **10 cases (26.32%)**
  - Drug-related adverse events: **3 cases (7.89%)**
  - Serious adverse events: **0 cases**
- **Placebo group (n=38)**
  - Adverse events: **12 cases (31.58%)**
  - Drug-related adverse events: **1 case (2.63%)**
  - Serious adverse events: **1 case (2.63%)**

There were **no statistically significant differences** between the groups, supporting a [[concepts/safety_profile|safety profile]] comparable to placebo in long-term use.

## Commonly reported reactions
The most frequent events in post-marketing reports were **gastrointestinal**, especially:
- heartburn
- dyspepsia
- epigastric discomfort
- upper abdominal pain

Other less frequent reactions included:
- edema
- headache
- rash
- leukopenia
- insomnia
- palpitations
- AST/ALT elevation
- dizziness

## Core ideas
### 1. Real-world safety assessment
Post-marketing surveillance reflects how a drug performs in routine clinical practice rather than in a tightly controlled trial setting.

### 2. Incidence and causality
Reports often distinguish between:
- **all reported adverse events**, and
- **events that cannot be ruled out as drug-related**

This distinction is important because not every observed event is necessarily caused by the medicine.

### 3. System-based categorization
Adverse reactions are commonly organized by body system, such as:
- gastrointestinal
- skin
- blood
- psychiatric
- hepatic/biliary
- nervous system

This helps clinicians recognize patterns and compare safety profiles across documents.

### 4. Surveillance and comparative trial evidence complement each other
Spontaneous or observational post-marketing data can reveal broader real-world patterns, while Phase 4 trials provide structured comparisons against placebo or other controls. Together, they strengthen the overall [[concepts/drug-safety]] assessment.
