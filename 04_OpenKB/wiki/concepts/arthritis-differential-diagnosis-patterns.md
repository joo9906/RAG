# Arthritis Differential Diagnosis Patterns

Arthritis differential diagnosis patterns refer to the recurring clinical clues used to distinguish major joint diseases based on **age of onset, symptom timing, joint distribution, symmetry, progression, and lab markers**. In this source, the contrast between [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] and [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] provides a clear example of how pattern recognition supports diagnosis.

## Core diagnostic pattern

Two common arthritis types can often be separated by whether the disease looks more **degenerative/mechanical** or **inflammatory/autoimmune**:

- **Degenerative pattern**: gradual onset, worse with use, localized, and more common in older adults
- **Inflammatory pattern**: faster onset, prolonged morning stiffness, symmetric small-joint involvement, and systemic laboratory inflammation

## Key distinctions from the source document

### Osteoarthritis pattern
- Usually appears **after age 50**
- Linked to **aging** and **joint overuse**
- Characterized by **cartilage damage** and **osteophyte** formation
- Develops **slowly over years**
- Morning stiffness is typically **under 30 minutes**
- Often **asymmetric**
- Mainly affects **weight-bearing joints**
- More **localized** than systemic
- **RF negative**
- **ESR** may rise only **temporarily**

### Rheumatoid arthritis pattern
- Can occur at **any age**, often **20–40 years**
- Caused by **autoimmune inflammation**
- Begins with **synovitis**
- Progresses **rapidly**
- Morning stiffness usually lasts **more than 1 hour**
- Often **symmetric**
- Commonly affects **small joints** such as the **hands and wrists**
- Can involve **systemic manifestations**
- **RF positive**
- **ESR** and **CRP elevated**

## Practical interpretation

These patterns help clinicians quickly decide whether joint symptoms are more consistent with [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] or [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]]. In practice, the most useful clues are:

1. **Age and onset**
2. **Morning stiffness duration**
3. **Symmetry vs. asymmetry**
4. **Small-joint vs. weight-bearing joint involvement**
5. **Inflammatory markers and RF status**

## Clinical takeaway

A short, asymmetric, mechanically related arthritis points toward OA, while a prolonged, symmetric, inflammatory pattern points toward RA. This makes arthritis differential diagnosis a pattern-based process rather than a single-test decision.
