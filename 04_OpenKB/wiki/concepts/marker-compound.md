# Marker Compound

## Definition
A **marker compound** is a chemically identified constituent present in a raw material or extract that is selected as a reference point for **quality management**. It is used to help verify identity, consistency, and overall batch quality.

## Role in Quality Control
Marker compounds are not always the sole active ingredients. Instead, they serve as measurable standards that support:

- **Material identification**
- **Batch-to-batch consistency**
- **Standardization of herbal products**
- **Quality assurance during manufacturing**

This concept is especially important in [[concepts/herbal-medicine]] and other multi-component natural products, where overall composition can vary depending on the source material and extraction process.

## Example from the Source Document
In [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0016]], Joins tablets (조인스정) are described as a natural-product drug made from extracts of:

- Clematis mandsurica (위령선)
- Trichosanthes kirilowii (괄루근)
- Prunella vulgaris (하고초)

The document states that Joins is managed using the marker compounds:

- **Oleanolic acid**
- **Rosmarinic acid**

These compounds are used to maintain consistent quality and efficacy across the product.

## Why Marker Compounds Matter
Marker compounds help bridge traditional or complex natural formulations with modern pharmaceutical quality standards. They provide a practical way to monitor products that contain many chemical constituents rather than a single purified active ingredient.
