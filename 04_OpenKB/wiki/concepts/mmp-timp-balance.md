# MMP-TIMP Balance in Cartilage Degradation

## Overview
The **MMP-TIMP balance** describes the regulatory relationship between **matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)** and **tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs)** in cartilage. In healthy tissue, this balance helps maintain cartilage integrity by keeping matrix synthesis and degradation in equilibrium. When the balance shifts toward excessive MMP activity, cartilage matrix breakdown increases and tissue damage progresses.

This concept is central to understanding cartilage degeneration described in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0004]] and is reinforced by [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]], which presents Joins as inhibiting MMP-related cartilage damage.

## Core Idea
Cartilage is maintained by a dynamic equilibrium between:
- **Synthesis** of cartilage matrix
- **Degradation** of cartilage matrix

MMPs promote degradation, while TIMPs suppress MMP activity. Damage occurs when MMP activity outweighs TIMP inhibition. This imbalance is a key mechanism in inflammatory and degenerative joint disease.

## Key Components

### MMPs
MMPs are the primary enzymes involved in cartilage breakdown.

Examples mentioned in the source:
- **MMP-1** — collagenase
- **MMP-2** — gelatinase
- **MMP-3** — stromelysin

These enzymes degrade components of the cartilage extracellular matrix and are presented in [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]] as a major target of Joins' cartilage-protective action.

### TIMPs
TIMPs inhibit MMPs and act as a protective counterforce.

Their role is to:
- limit excessive matrix degradation
- preserve cartilage structure
- help maintain tissue homeostasis

### Cytokines
Inflammatory cytokines such as **IL-1** and **TNF-α** support MMP activity.

In the source documents, cytokines are presented as factors that enhance the degradative pathway, indirectly contributing to cartilage damage. In particular, [[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]] highlights **IL-1** as a key upstream mediator that promotes cartilage injury by supporting MMP activity, inducing nitric oxide (NO), and contributing to cell death.

### Oxidative stress and cell loss
The newer source also expands the degradative framework beyond enzyme balance alone:
- **Free radicals** are described as highly reactive and pathogenic in arthritis
- **Chondrocyte apoptosis** reduces the cells responsible for cartilage maintenance

These processes reinforce the MMP-TIMP imbalance by weakening cartilage repair capacity and accelerating matrix loss.

## Mechanism of Cartilage Degradation
1. Cytokines such as IL-1 and TNF-α increase or support MMP activity.
2. Free radicals contribute to inflammatory and degenerative stress in cartilage.
3. MMPs break down cartilage matrix proteins.
4. TIMPs attempt to inhibit MMPs.
5. If inhibition is insufficient, the balance shifts toward degradation.
6. Chondrocyte apoptosis further reduces cartilage maintenance and repair.
7. Cartilage tissue is progressively damaged.

## Biological Significance
The MMP-TIMP balance is important because cartilage has limited regenerative capacity. Once degradation exceeds repair, structural damage can accumulate and impair joint function.

This makes the MMP-TIMP axis a useful framework for studying:
- cartilage degeneration
- inflammatory joint damage
- tissue remodeling and regeneration
- oxidative and cytokine-driven cartilage injury

## Therapeutic Relevance
[[summaries/2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0018]] suggests that Joins may help preserve cartilage by targeting multiple steps in this destructive cascade:
- free radical inhibition
- IL-1 inhibition
- MMP inhibition
- chondrocyte apoptosis inhibition

This supports the idea that maintaining MMP-TIMP balance is not only about direct enzyme inhibition, but also about reducing upstream inflammatory and oxidative drivers that push the system toward degradation.
