# Summary: Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Symptoms

This document outlines the main symptom patterns of [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis (RA)]], focusing on prodromal symptoms, characteristic joint involvement, and extra-articular manifestations.

## Key points

### Prodromal symptoms
- In about 20% of patients, RA begins suddenly with systemic symptoms.
- Early symptoms may include:
  - fever
  - polyarthritis
  - depression and anxiety
  - fatigue
  - loss of appetite
  - weight loss

### Joint involvement
- RA most commonly affects the small joints of the hands, wrists, and feet.
- Other joints that may also be involved include:
  - elbows
  - shoulders
  - hips
  - knees
  - ankles
- A hallmark symptom is [[concepts/morning-stiffness|morning stiffness]]:
  - usually worst in the morning
  - typically lasts more than 30 minutes
  - may persist for 1 hour or longer, or even all day
- Common joint findings include:
  - pain
  - swelling
  - tenderness

### Extra-articular involvement
- RA can affect organs and tissues beyond the joints.
- Examples listed in the document include:
  - vasculitis
  - pulmonary fibrosis
  - conjunctivitis
  - pericarditis
  - lymphadenitis

## Takeaway
RA is presented as a systemic inflammatory disease with possible abrupt onset, prominent small-joint involvement, prolonged morning stiffness, and multi-organ extra-articular disease.
