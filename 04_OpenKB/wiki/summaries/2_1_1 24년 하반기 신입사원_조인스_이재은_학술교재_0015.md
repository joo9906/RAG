# Summary

This document provides a side-by-side comparison of **osteoarthritis (OA)** and **rheumatoid arthritis (RA)**, focusing on their typical patient profiles, disease mechanisms, symptom patterns, laboratory findings, and progression.

## Key comparison

### Osteoarthritis (OA)
- Commonly occurs in **older adults, especially after age 50**.
- Associated with **aging** and **joint overuse**.
- Main pathology is **cartilage damage** with **osteophyte formation**.
- Progresses **gradually over years**.
- Morning stiffness is usually **less than 30 minutes**.
- Symptoms are often **asymmetric** and mainly involve **weight-bearing joints**.
- Tends to remain **localized** rather than systemic.
- **RF** is typically **negative**.
- **ESR** may be **transiently elevated**.

### Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)
- Can occur at **any age**, especially **20–40 years**.
- Caused by an **autoimmune process**.
- Primary pathology begins with **synovitis**.
- Progresses **rapidly** compared with OA.
- Morning stiffness is usually **more than 1 hour**.
- Symptoms are often **symmetric**.
- Frequently affects **small joints** such as the **fingers and wrists**.
- Can involve the **whole body/systemic features**.
- **RF** is often **positive**.
- **ESR** and **CRP** are typically **elevated**.

## Main takeaway

The central distinction is that [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] is a **degenerative, mechanically driven joint disease**, while [[concepts/rheumatoid-arthritis]] is a **systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease**. Their differences in onset age, stiffness duration, joint distribution, symmetry, and laboratory markers are key for clinical differentiation.
