# Summary: Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) Treatment

This document outlines the treatment goals and major medication options for **rheumatoid arthritis (RA)**, focusing on symptom control, prevention of joint damage, and long-term disease management.

## Key Treatment Goals

- Prevent and control **joint damage**
- Suppress **inflammatory responses** to prevent complications
- Maintain daily function, improve **quality of life**, and reduce mortality

## Main Drug Therapies

### NSAIDs ([[concepts/NSAIDs]])
- Used to reduce **pain** and decrease **inflammation**
- Primarily for symptomatic relief rather than disease modification

### Steroids ([[concepts/corticosteroids]])
- Provide a strong **anti-inflammatory** effect
- Useful for controlling active inflammation

### DMARDs ([[concepts/DMARDs]])
- **Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs** that help induce remission or slow disease progression
- Early use is associated with better outcomes
- Examples mentioned:
  - methotrexate
  - sulfasalazine
  - leflunomide

### Biologic Agents ([[concepts/biologic-therapy]])
- Such as **TNF inhibitors**, which block inflammatory mediators involved in RA
- Help suppress the inflammatory process more specifically
- Examples mentioned:
  - infliximab
  - adalimumab

## Core Takeaway

RA management is centered on **early and appropriate pharmacologic treatment** to relieve symptoms, control inflammation, and prevent irreversible joint damage. Among the options listed, **DMARDs and biologic agents** are key for modifying disease course, while **NSAIDs and steroids** mainly help with symptom and inflammation control.
