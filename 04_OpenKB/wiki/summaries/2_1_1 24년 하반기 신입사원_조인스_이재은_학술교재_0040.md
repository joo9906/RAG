# Summary: Osteoarthritis prevalence in Korean adults

This document compiles prevalence statistics for osteoarthritis (OA) by body site in Korean adults, focusing on population-based studies that distinguish between symptoms, radiographic OA, and their overlap. It highlights clear sex differences and shows that prevalence varies substantially by joint and age group.

## Key findings

### 1) National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (2010–2012)
- Cross-sectional study of 8,976 adults aged 50 years or older.
- Sample included 3,830 men and 5,146 women.
- **Symptomatic spinal OA** was present in:
  - **5.6% of men**
  - **16.0% of women**
- Overall, women showed higher proportions of symptom-related and combined symptom+radiographic OA across major sites.

#### Site-specific prevalence patterns
- **Hip**: relatively low radiographic OA, but symptoms were more common in women than men.
- **Knee**: one of the most common OA sites, especially in women.
- **Spine**: high prevalence of both symptoms and radiographic OA, with the highest symptomatic overlap in women.

#### Definitions used
- **Sx**: symptomatic only
- **OA**: radiographic features only
- **Sx+OA**: both symptoms and radiographic features present

These categories are useful for understanding the gap between structural disease and clinical burden in [[concepts/osteoarthritis]].

### 2) Cross-sectional OA prevalence study in older adults
- Cross-sectional study of 1,118 adults aged 65 years or older.
- Radiographic OA prevalence was highest in the **spine**, followed by the **hand**, **knee**, **shoulder**, and **hip**.
- Overall prevalence among evaluated participants:
  - **Spine**: 66.4% (462/696)
  - **Hand**: 60.0% (415/692)
  - **Knee**: 38.1% (265/696)
  - **Shoulder**: 5.2% (36/696)
  - **Hip**: 2.2% (15/686)

#### Sex differences
- **Women** had higher prevalence of **hand** and **knee** OA.
- **Men** had slightly higher prevalence of **spinal** OA in some comparisons.
- The study underscores that OA burden is not uniform across joints or between sexes, supporting a broader view of [[concepts/joint-specific osteoarthritis]] and [[concepts/sex differences in disease prevalence]].

## Interpretation
- OA is highly prevalent in older Korean adults, especially in the spine, hand, and knee.
- The distinction between symptoms and radiographic findings is important because structural OA does not always match clinical symptoms.
- Sex-specific patterns suggest the need for tailored screening, prevention, and management strategies.
- The document is most relevant for epidemiology, musculoskeletal health, and population-level burden estimation.
