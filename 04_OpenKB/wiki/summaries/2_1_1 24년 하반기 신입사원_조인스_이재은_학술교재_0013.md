# Summary: 류마티스관절염(RA) 진단 and Laboratory Interpretation

This document explains the basic diagnostic framework for **rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** and the role of common laboratory tests in evaluation and monitoring. It emphasizes that RA diagnosis is not based on a single test, but on a combination of clinical findings and laboratory evidence.

## Key points

### RA classification/diagnosis
- RA is diagnosed by summing points across four categories:
  1. **Joint involvement pattern**
  2. **Serologic tests**
  3. **Acute-phase reactants** such as ESR and CRP
  4. **Symptom duration**
- A total score of **6 points or more** supports a diagnosis of RA.

### Rheumatoid factor (RF)
- **Rheumatoid factor** is positive in about **80%** of RA patients.
- However, about **5% of healthy individuals** may also test positive.
- Therefore, a positive RF result **does not by itself confirm RA**.
- Higher RF levels are associated with a tendency toward **more severe joint damage**.

### Other laboratory tests
- **CBC, liver function tests, and kidney function tests** are used to check for:
  - adverse effects from treatment
  - involvement of other organs
- **ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate)** and **CRP (C-reactive protein)** are used to assess the **degree of inflammation**.
- Elevated ESR/CRP is commonly seen when inflammation is active.

## Clinical takeaway
- RA diagnosis requires a **composite assessment** rather than relying on RF alone.
- Inflammatory markers such as **ESR** and **CRP** help gauge disease activity, while routine blood chemistry helps monitor **treatment safety** and systemic effects.
