# Summary: 2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0043

## Main topic
This document compares the clinical usefulness of **JOINS®** and **Imodium®** for **knee osteoarthritis (OA)**, focusing on evidence for **cartilage protection** and **long-term administration**.

## Key claim
- **JOINS®** has clinical evidence from a **2-year phase 4 study** suggesting cartilage-protective effects in knee OA.
- **Imodium®** lacks comparable clinical evidence in knee OA, and most available studies are only **3 to 6 months** long, leaving **insufficient evidence for treatment beyond 1 year**.

## JOINS® evidence
The document states that JOINS® demonstrated cartilage-protective effects in a **phase 4 randomized controlled trial (RCT)** in patients with knee OA.

### Study summary
- **Study type:** RCT
- **Purpose:** Compare the clinical effect of JOINS® on cartilage protection versus placebo using MRI
- **Participants:** Osteoarthritis patients, **n = 69**
- **Treatment:** JOINS 200 mg tid vs placebo
- **Duration:** **12 months + 12 months**
- **Endpoints:** dGEMRIC, subarticular-BME, VAS, K-WOMAC
- **Conclusion:** JOINS showed superior clinical effect for cartilage protection compared with placebo

## Imodium® evidence
The document argues that Imodium® does not have clinical data demonstrating cartilage protection in knee OA.

### Evidence limitations
- No clinical study specifically evaluating cartilage protection in knee OA is presented
- Most studies are short-term, lasting **3 to 6 months**
- Evidence is insufficient for **long-term use beyond 1 year**

### Hip OA studies mentioned
Two hip OA studies are cited as supporting context, but they do not establish clear structural benefit:
- **Lequesne (2002):** Did not prove structural improvement versus placebo
- **Maheu (2014):** No significant difference versus placebo in the primary endpoint of **joint space width (JSW)**, though the proportion of patients with JSW worsening was reduced

## Interpretation
The document positions **JOINS®** as having stronger evidence for **[[concepts/cartilage_protection]]** in knee OA, especially for **long-term treatment**, while **Imodium®** is described as lacking sufficient evidence for the same claim.
