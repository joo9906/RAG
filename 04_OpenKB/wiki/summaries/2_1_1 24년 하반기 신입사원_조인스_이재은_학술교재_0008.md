# Summary: 2_1_1 24년 하반기 신입사원_조인스_이재은_학술교재_0008

## Main topic
This document summarizes how to diagnose **osteoarthritis (OA)** by combining the patient's **medical history**, **physical examination**, and **imaging findings** such as **X-ray** and **MRI**.

## Key points
- OA diagnosis is not based on a single test.
- Clinicians integrate:
  - detailed patient history
  - clinical symptoms and signs
  - physical examination findings
  - radiographic and MRI evidence
- Early OA may show **normal X-ray findings**, so diagnosis can be challenging at first.

## Imaging findings in osteoarthritis

### X-ray findings
- Early-stage OA may appear normal.
- As disease progresses, **joint space narrowing** becomes visible.
- **Subchondral sclerosis** may develop, appearing as increased bone density beneath the cartilage.
- In more advanced disease, **osteophytes** form at the joint margins and the joint surface becomes irregular.

### MRI findings
- MRI can assess:
  - cartilage thickness
  - cartilage volume
  - biochemical composition of cartilage
- This makes MRI useful for observing early cartilage change.
- However, MRI use is limited by **cost** and **time**.

## Clinical takeaway
OA diagnosis relies on a **comprehensive assessment** rather than imaging alone. X-ray is useful for structural changes in later stages, while MRI provides more detailed cartilage evaluation but is less practical for routine use.
