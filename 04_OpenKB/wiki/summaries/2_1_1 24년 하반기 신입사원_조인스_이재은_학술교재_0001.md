# Summary

This document provides a basic explanation of the structure of joints and their main components to support understanding of osteoarthritis (OA). It introduces the joint as the site where two or more bones connect and describes the roles of cartilage, ligaments, synovial membrane, and synovial fluid.

## Key Concepts

### Joint
A joint is the area where two or more bones meet. It is made up of several structures that work together to enable movement and protect the bones.

### Main Components of a Joint
- **Cartilage**: Covers the ends of bones and protects the joint surface.
- **Ligament**: Helps connect bones and controls joint movement.
- **Synovial membrane**: Surrounds and connects the bone ends within the joint; it contains blood vessels and nerves and secretes synovial fluid.
- **Synovial fluid**: Fills the joint cavity, provides lubrication, and reduces friction.

## Relevance to Osteoarthritis
The document frames these structures as foundational anatomy for understanding [[concepts/osteoarthritis]] and how joint components contribute to normal function and joint protection.

## Takeaway
The core message is that healthy joint function depends on the coordinated roles of cartilage, ligaments, synovial membrane, and synovial fluid.
