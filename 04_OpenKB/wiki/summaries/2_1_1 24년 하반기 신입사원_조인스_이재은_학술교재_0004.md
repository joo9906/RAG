# 조인스 — Summary

## Overview
This document explains the biochemical mechanism of **cartilage damage and regeneration** by focusing on the balance between **MMPs**, **TIMPs**, and **cytokines**. It presents cartilage as a dynamic system in which synthesis and degradation continuously interact.

## Key Concepts

### Cartilage synthesis vs. degradation
- Cartilage maintenance depends on a balance between:
  - **Cartilage synthesizing** processes
  - **Cartilage degradation** processes
- When this balance is disrupted, cartilage tissue is damaged.

### MMPs (matrix metalloproteinases)
- **MMPs** are described as the primary enzymes responsible for cartilage breakdown.
- Specific examples mentioned:
  - **MMP-1**: collagenase
  - **MMP-2**: gelatinase
  - **MMP-3**: stromelysin
- These enzymes contribute directly to the degradation of the cartilage matrix.

### TIMPs (tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases)
- **TIMPs** inhibit MMP activity.
- They function as a protective regulatory mechanism that limits excessive matrix degradation.
- The interaction between **MMPs** and **TIMPs** is central to cartilage homeostasis.

### Cytokines
- The cytokines mentioned are **IL-1** and **TNF-α**.
- These molecules promote cartilage degradation by supporting the action of MMPs.
- In the document, cytokines are presented as auxiliary factors that help MMP function.

## Main Mechanism
- Cartilage damage occurs when the balance between **MMPs** and **TIMPs** is broken.
- Increased MMP activity, supported by cytokines, leads to **cartilage tissue destruction**.
- Proper regulation of this balance is important for preserving cartilage integrity and supporting regeneration.

## Takeaway
The document emphasizes that cartilage injury is not caused by a single factor, but by an imbalance among **matrix-degrading enzymes**, **their inhibitors**, and **inflammatory cytokines**. This makes the **MMP–TIMP–cytokine axis** a key concept for understanding cartilage degeneration and repair.
