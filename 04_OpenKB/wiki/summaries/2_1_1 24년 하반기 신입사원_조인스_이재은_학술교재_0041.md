# Summary: 조인스 — 척추증 (Spondylosis)

## Overview
This document explains **spondylosis**, also called **spinal osteoarthritis**, including its definition, causes, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment options. It also discusses the potential applicability of **조인스® (Joins)** for patients with spinal osteoarthritis based on its approved indications and related OA/RA research.

## Key Concepts
- **Spondylosis**: A degenerative change in the spine, classified as spinal osteoarthritis.
- **Degeneration of spinal structures**: Aging-related damage to bones, discs, cartilage, and ligaments.
- **Symptom variability**: Some patients are asymptomatic, while others experience back pain and stiffness.
- **Spinal stenosis risk**: Spondylosis can narrow the spinal canal and compress the spinal cord or nerve roots.
- **Treatment goals**: Reduce pain, improve function, and enhance quality of life.
- **Joins® relevance**: Although no direct clinical study in spondylosis is cited, the document suggests it may be prescribed within its approved use and may help reduce pain and support cartilage protection.

## Definition
Spondylosis is described as **spinal osteoarthritis** and a form of **degenerative spinal change**. It reflects progressive wear-and-tear in spinal tissues.

## Causes
The main cause is **aging-related degeneration**. Over time, the following structures may be damaged:
- Bones
- Intervertebral discs
- Cartilage
- Ligaments

This gradual deterioration contributes to structural changes in the spine.

## Symptoms
Symptoms may vary widely:
- Some people have **no noticeable symptoms**.
- When symptoms occur, the most common are:
  - **Low back pain**
  - **Stiffness**

The document also notes that spondylosis can lead to **spinal stenosis**, where narrowing of the spinal canal compresses the spinal cord or spinal nerve roots. This may cause:
- Numbness
- Tingling
- Muscle weakness

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis is generally based on:
- Medical history
- Physical examination
- Imaging studies

Imaging methods mentioned:
- **X-ray**: Helps assess the degree of arthritis or bone damage
- **MRI**: Evaluates the spinal cord and surrounding nerves, including nerve compression
- **CT**: Examines the spinal canal, bones, and joints

## Treatment
The treatment objective is to **relieve pain, improve function, and improve quality of life**.

### Non-drug treatments
- Exercise
- Weight loss
- Massage
- Warm compresses / heat therapy

### Drug treatments
- **Acetaminophen**
- **NSAIDs** (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)
- Topical ointments and creams
- Mild narcotics
- Steroid injections

### Surgery
Surgery may be considered in more severe cases.

## Joins® Applicability
The document states that **Joins® has no direct clinical study in spondylosis**, but it may still be prescribed for spinal osteoarthritis according to its approved labeling. The rationale is based on findings from OA and RA studies suggesting potential benefits in:
- Pain reduction
- Inhibiting structural joint degeneration

This positions Joins as a product of interest in the context of [[concepts/osteoarthritis]], [[concepts/degenerative-disease]], and [[concepts/pain-management]].

## Notable Takeaway
The core message is that spondylosis is a common degenerative spine disorder with a broad spectrum of symptoms and standard conservative-to-surgical treatment options, and that Joins® may have a relevant role despite the absence of direct spondylosis-specific clinical evidence.
