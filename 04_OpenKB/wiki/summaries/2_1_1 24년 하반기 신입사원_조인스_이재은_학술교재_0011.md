# Summary: Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

This document introduces **rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** as a **chronic systemic inflammatory autoimmune disease** in which the immune system attacks the joints, especially the **synovium**. The key clinical and pathological theme is that persistent synovial inflammation progresses to **cartilage damage**, **bone erosion**, and eventually **joint fusion and stiffness**.

## Core definition

- **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** is an autoimmune disorder.
- The immune system targets the body’s own joints.
- The disease is marked by **ongoing inflammatory synovitis** in the joint lining.
- It is described as a **chronic inflammatory systemic disease**.

## Disease progression

The document outlines the typical pathological sequence:

1. **Inflammatory synovitis**
2. **Cartilage destruction**
3. **Joint fusion and stiffness**

This progression highlights how inflammation in the synovium can evolve into structural joint damage over time.

## Pannus formation

A central concept in RA pathology is **pannus**:

- Pannus is a thickened mass of synovial tissue.
- It develops due to **synovial cell proliferation** and infiltration by inflammatory cells.
- The pannus grows into adjacent tissues.
- It invades cartilage and causes **erosion of cartilage and bone**.
- This invasion is a major mechanism of **joint destruction** in RA.

## Key takeaway

The document emphasizes that RA is not just joint pain or swelling, but a **self-directed immune process** in which chronic synovial inflammation and pannus formation drive progressive and irreversible joint damage.
